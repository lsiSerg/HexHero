## Project status

HexHero is an Unreal Engine 5.8 C++ project. It started from the top-down template, whose code and content have been removed and remain in Git history. The hex world map and its camera exist; all other game systems are still to come.

## Game concept

A prototype for exploring UE and the developer's own skills, so favour learning and working results over production-scale systems.

- Turn-based strategy in the spirit of Heroes 3, with the developer's own changes.
- The world is the hex planet; it is no longer only a prototype. It is up to about 256 x 256 hexes (frequency 80). Mountains and other blockers make it a maze rather than open fields, so players are kept apart and must find a way.
- Resources: gold, ore, mercury, sulfur, crystals and wood, each with mines. A captured mine yields its resource every turn.
- Heroes gain experience by winning battles and from chests, which offer a choice of gold or experience.
- A hero can travel a set distance each turn. Some objects add to it for the next turn or the week, and some biomes cost more to cross.
- The map contains castles, mines, chests, hostile units and heroes.
- A castle is either neutral or owned by the player who captured it.
- Units are hostile to everyone, except that a unit a player placed on the map is friendly to that player, who can take it back into their army.
- Attacking a unit or hero opens a separate small world of about 15x11 hexes. It is not generated: it is a hex grid laid over an authored 3D environment, where decor can be added to style a map. There the attacking hero's units fight the units from the world map.
- An underground: entrances on the planet join in networks underground, and a hero who enters goes to it through a fade to black. It is a maze of passages between the entrances, not an open map, at least two cells wide, and between them an abyss in a teal grey mist where nothing moves. It has no mountains but cave decor such as stalactites, and an underground mood. It is a second hex sphere of the same frequency as the planet, each of its cells under the surface cell of the same index (2026-11-08), so the grid and editor of the planet serve it. It is seen from outside as a globe for now; the view from inside, with the camera looking at its inner surface as chosen on 2026-11-06, comes later, its far side hidden by a stone vault above the camera (decided on 2026-11-09).
- Ice at sea is passable, as all water is: floes of many sizes, from big slabs to small shards, float on open water between them, drift slowly and grind together, denser in the cold. There are no impassable ice shelves (changed on 2026-11-06).
- Multiplayer is wanted.

Performance target: the game must run on modest gaming PCs, ideally at 60 FPS with everything on screen (gameplay, resources, the trees and other objects that will fill the map). Before relying on a feature of high-end GPUs, check that it has a cheaper fallback or can be turned off, so the project does not end in a dead end. What relies on them now and needs that review: Lumen with hardware ray tracing (`r.RayTracing=True`), virtual shadow maps, Substrate, Nanite mountains, the heavy per-pixel water shaders, and terrain without LODs. The planet was measured on 2026-11-05 at 1080p on an RTX 4080 Super: about 3.2 to 4.4 ms of GPU, the most in shadow depths (about 0.6 to 0.9 ms), clouds and water (up to 0.7 ms) and TSR (0.55 ms, TAA 0.07); plain shadow maps, no Lumen or ray tracing and TAA together saved about 1.3 ms. On 2026-11-07, with the sky light, the haze and the forests, at 1280x720: 3.86 ms of GPU over a dense forest with virtual shadow maps and 3.76 with cascaded ones (`r.Shadow.Virtual.Enable 0`, which draws 8.2 million triangles against 3.5 and softer shadows), 4.15 and 3.98 at mid distance, so plain shadow maps work as the cheaper fallback; there is no menu of quality yet.

Not decided yet, so ask before building on any of these:
- the light of the game on the surface: whether the sun follows the camera, as now, or day and night change by turns. The underground was decided on 2026-11-10: it is dark, its maze dim but readable without peering from any distance, from afar mostly what glows showing, and it is lit around the hero (for now the focus of the camera, one day as far as the hero can go in a turn), by what glows and at the exits (the dark underground, below); whether the palette of the brush should lift the dark while painting was asked and not answered, so it changes nothing yet;
- the view from inside the underground, which comes after the view from outside that is built now: on 2026-11-09 the developer decided that it hides its far side with a stone vault, a smaller sphere above the camera that serves as the ceiling of the cave and can carry stalactites, with the camera always below it; it is not built yet, and how the camera and the light work inside and when the vault comes are still open; its size (1:1, the frequency of the planet), its voids (an abyss in a teal grey mist) and passages at least two cells wide are decided;
- how multiplayer works (hot seat, network) and whether there are AI opponents;
- how battles play out in detail;
- the exact rules of movement and the bonuses.

## Architecture

There is one runtime module, `HexHero`, in `Source/HexHero`.

### Hex world map

The code lives in folders of `Source/HexHero`, and the level and materials in `Content/HexMap`. `Lvl_HexWorld` is the default map.

- `Core`: game mode and project settings.
- `Grid`: hex coordinates and layout, shared by any hex grid.
- `Map`: world map actor, mesh building, map loading and the map data assets.
- `Camera`: camera pawn and its data asset.
- `Player`: player controller.
- `UI`: Slate widgets.
- `Tests`: automation tests.
- `Planet`: prototype of the world map as a hex planet, apart from everything above.

A data asset class stays next to the code that uses it. Every meaningful value that can be tuned for the result, also in the Custom HLSL of materials, belongs in a data asset, as far as it costs no performance; obvious math constants stay in code.

### Planet prototype

The world map is meant to become a planet, so this prototype tries the sphere before anything else moves to it. `Lvl_HexPlanet` in `Content/HexPlanet` uses `AHexPlanetGameMode` as its override; open it with `/Game/HexPlanet/Lvl_HexPlanet?Planet=<folder>` in place of the map in the run command, or with the buttons of the planets in `SHexMapMenu`, and `MapMenuAction` there shows the map menu over the planet (`AHexPlanetPlayerController::OpenMapMenu`), so no map loads until one is chosen, which opens it in `Lvl_HexWorld`, or a planet, which opens `Lvl_HexPlanet` again with it. Nothing cooks it yet. `Tools/create_planet_assets.py` creates that level, `M_HexPlanet`, `M_HexPlanetWater`, `M_HexPlanetWaterGrid`, `M_HexPlanetWireframe` and the materials of the underground, so edit the script and not them; with the argument `configs` (`-script="<file> configs"`) it only makes the data assets that are missing, and with `underground` only the materials of the underground, of the cave objects (`M_HexPlanetCave`) and of the motes (`M_HexPlanetMote`), leaving the rest as it is, and `underground markers` those of the markers of entrances too, which otherwise are not saved again with the same graphs, as that only churns their binary assets; an existing `DA_HexUnderground` takes the values of `UNDERGROUND_MIGRATIONS` there only while it still holds their old values, so tuning by hand stays.

- A planet is a folder in `Content/Data/Planets`, read at runtime as the maps in `Content/Data/Maps` (`HexPlanetFile`): `planet.json` (`FHexPlanetManifest`: version, name, description, `frequency`, `seed`, `sortOrder`, `generation`, and `underground`, the generation of the underground) and an optional `levels.txt`, one letter per cell in the order of `FHexSphereGrid` (D deep sea, S shallows, P plains, H hills, M mountains) in lines of 80, so an edit changes only the lines of its cells, and an optional `biomes.txt` in the same layout, the `PlanetLetter` of the biome of each cell (`HexPlanetFile::EncodeBiomes`); without it, or when it is not valid, `HexPlanetGenerator::GenerateLevels` generates them from the seed and `generation` (`FHexPlanetGeneration`), and a saved planet that lacked either file writes what was generated at once (`AHexPlanet::WriteGeneratedFiles`), so a planet never changes when the generation does later (a file that is there but not valid is left alone), and a pentagon that the file makes a mountain becomes hills. Its `start` is `Random`, `Water` (deep sea) or `Land` (plains); a random planet ranks the cells by a noise of levels (`LevelNoiseScale` of `DA_HexPlanet` divided by `continentSize`, `coastDetail` octaves) and splits them by shares, highest first, so a share holds on any planet: `landShare` of the cells is land and `shallowsShare` of the sea shallows, `mountainShare` of the land is mountains, the land furthest from the coast (`RangeMinLand`) ranked by where a second noise nears zero and how far inland it lies, and `hillShare` of the land is hills, the highest of the rest. Values of scale and speed are tuned on a planet of `ReferenceFrequency` of `DA_HexPlanet` (80, Terra), and on another planet scale by its frequency over that one (`UHexPlanetConfig::GetSizeRatio`), so they keep their size in cells: the ranges (`RangeNoiseScale`, `RangeWobbleScale`), so a smaller planet has fewer ranges as wide, and the clouds, whose `CloudScale` `AHexPlanet::SetCloudParameters` multiplies and whose `CloudSpeed`, a turn, it divides by the ratio, so a smaller planet has fewer clouds of the same size moving as fast over the ground. The defaults of `FHexPlanetGeneration` give `Terra` exactly as the old thresholds did. "New hex planet..." in `SHexMapMenu` shows `SHexNewPlanetPanel` (size, start, seed, shares, continent size, coast detail), whose Create opens the planet without writing anything (`HexPlanetFile::OpenNewPlanet` keeps it in memory and opens the level with `?NewPlanet`), so trying planets leaves no files. Such a planet has no folder (`AHexPlanet::IsSaved`), the overlay warns "Planet not saved", and `SaveViewAction` asks for its name in an `SHexDialog` (`AHexPlanetPlayerController::OpenSavePlanetDialog`) before it saves the view: the folder is the name with anything but letters of any alphabet, digits, - and _ made _ (`SanitizeFolderName`), a folder that is there already needs a second press of Overwrite, and `AHexPlanet::SaveAs` writes `planet.json`, `levels.txt`, `biomes.txt` and the files of the underground (see below). Leaving a planet from the menu for a map, another planet or a new one while it is new or has painted cells not saved (`AHexPlanet::HasUnsavedChanges`) asks first (`AHexPlanetPlayerController::LeavePlanet`): save, which leaves once saved, leave without saving, or stay; the console commands `hex.LoadPlanet` and `hex.LoadMap` do not ask. The main page of the menu has Save planet (`OnSave`, `CanSave`), enabled only while there is something to save, which saves as the save key does (`AHexPlanetCameraPawn::SavePlanet`) and closes the menu only for the dialog of the name of a new planet. `CopyCameraAction` on such a planet asks first (`OpenCopyCameraDialog`): save, copy only `hex.PlanetSetCamera`, or cancel. A saved planet saves only the view, as before. A dialog gives the keyboard back to the game when it closes (`SetAllUserFocusToGameViewport`), as a text box committed with Enter would otherwise keep it. New planets go to `Content/Data/Planets` in builds with the editor, so they can be committed, and to `Saved/Planets` in a packaged game, whose Content is read only; `FindPlanets` lists both. Cells have the same size on every planet (`HexRadius`), so the frequency sets the radius: 20 is about 63 x 63 hexes, 32 about 100 x 100, 48 about 150 x 150, 64 about 200 x 200, and 80 about 256 x 256. `AHexPlanet` opens the planet of the `?Planet=` option of the URL (`HexPlanetFile::OpenPlanet`), else the one saved with the view, else the lowest `sortOrder`. `Terra` is the planet of the prototype and `Islet` a small one with more sea, both generated. Folder names must have no spaces, as `hex.LoadPlanet` takes one as an argument.
- Every value of the planet that can be tuned is in a data asset in `Content/HexPlanet`, which `create_planet_assets.py` creates only when missing, so tune them by hand: `DA_HexPlanet` (`UHexPlanetConfig`: size of the cells, generation of levels and ranges, sea, water cap, colors, outlines, and the values of `M_HexPlanet` and `M_HexPlanetWireframe`), `DA_HexPlanetWater` (`UHexPlanetWaterConfig`: every value of `WATER_CODE` and `WATER_OFFSET_CODE`, a wave as (frequency, speed, slope, height)), `DA_HexPlanetClouds` and `DA_HexPlanetCamera` (`UHexPlanetCameraConfig`, read by `AHexPlanetCameraPawn::Settings`). A missing one falls back to the defaults of its class with a warning. `HexMaterialConfig::Apply` gives a material every float, int (as a float), color, `FVector` and `FVector4` of a config as parameters of the same names, once when it is made, and the script names the ones each material takes (`add_config_parameters`, defaults from the class), so a new value needs only the property and the parameter. A parameter for an `FVector4` goes through an append node, as a vector parameter gives a pin only its RGB.
- `FHexSphereGrid` is a Goldberg polyhedron: each face of an icosahedron is split into `Frequency` steps, so it has 10 * Frequency^2 + 2 cells, and the first 12 are the pentagons. Seen from outside, neighbors and corners go counterclockwise, corner K lies between neighbor K and K + 1, and the side towards neighbor K runs from corner K - 1 to corner K; three cells share each corner. The points of a face blend its vertices by sines, which keeps the largest hex within 1.2 of the smallest, where a straight projection gives 1.9. Relaxing the lattice by springs or by moving to the middle of the neighbors made it worse. `FindCell` walks to nearer neighbors.
- `HexPlanetSurface` builds the surface: one `UHexPlanetMeshComponent` per chunk (`SurfaceChunks`, under the empty root `Surface`) with two sections, so what lies behind the planet or off the screen is culled and a rebuild recreates the render state of its chunks alone. That component keeps a section on the GPU alone (`PrepareSection` packs it on any thread, `SetSection` sends it and the CPU frees it there): a position, packed tangents and four UVs of half floats, 36 bytes a vertex (the walls of the underground have a fifth, 40), no vertex colors, indices of 16 bits where they fit, and ray tracing geometry built to be traced fast and compacted. Its render data outlives the scene proxies, so a change of material or visibility uploads nothing; it draws as a static mesh, with cached mesh draw commands, and gives ray tracing its sections as a dynamic mesh does, referencing a geometry the manager evicted so it comes back. `UProceduralMeshComponent`, used before, held each section three times (152 bytes a vertex in the component, a copy in its scene proxy, one on the GPU) and built its ray tracing geometry fast and uncompacted, 54 bytes a triangle where it is now 26. The build hands its sections over in floats (`FSection`), as the GPU takes them, and finds them in doubles. A chunk (`FHexSphereCell::Chunk`) is a triangle of a face of the icosahedron split into about `ChunkSteps` of `DA_HexPlanet` lattice steps along a side (`FHexSphereGrid::Build`, the `Grid.Chunks` test); 27 gives Terra 180 chunks of about 360 cells, which drew as few triangles as 320 chunks with a third fewer draw calls, and from close to the planet about 1.6 million triangles of 6 where the 20 faces drew 5.5. Each chunk also has a coarse mesh (`CoarseChunks`, `HexPlanetSurface::FBuild::CoarseSections`), a fan of 12 triangles a cell from its center, corners and side middles at the heights of the nearest vertices of its piece there (`FCoarsePiece`), placed as the piece, so 0.8 million triangles for the whole of Terra: beyond `CoarseDistance` of `DA_HexPlanet` from the camera it is drawn in place of the pieces. `AHexPlanet::UpdateChunkLod` shows each chunk by the distance of its bounds every tick (`ChunkLodStates`): the pieces where all of it is nearer than `CoarseBand` short of `CoarseDistance`, the coarse mesh where all of it is further than `CoarseBand` past it, and both where it reaches into that band, with the masked `LodNearMaterial` and `LodFarMaterial`, copies of `M_HexPlanet_Cut` with `LodSide` 1 and 2, whose mask (`SURFACE_CUT_CODE`) keeps complementary pixels by a noise of the pixel that changes every frame against how far each point lies across the band from `LodCamera`, the camera, so the temporal filter dissolves one into the other without a pop and every pixel is drawn once; only the chunks in the band pay for the masked material, which on Terra at mid distance cost about 0.2 ms of GPU. `hex.PlanetCoarseDistance` and `hex.PlanetCoarseBand` move the band while the game runs, for trying it closer, and 0 goes back to the values of the asset. The whole planet in view then draws about 1.6 million triangles where the pieces drew 9, and its GPU time on the machine it was made on fell from 4.5 to 3.2 ms at 1080p. So only the chunks near the camera keep their pieces (`ChunkPieces`): one whose nearest point comes within `PieceStreamAhead` of `DA_HexPlanet` past the band has them built in the background from what the surface shown was built from (`StartStream`, a build of the pieces alone with the heights of its corners and relief given, `FBuildOptions`, 6 to 35 ms a chunk on Terra, `PieceStreamChunks` at a time), one further than `PieceStreamKeep` past that frees them, and a chunk without them draws its coarse mesh; a rebuild of levels gives pieces only to the chunks that want them and drops what a stream built of a chunk it made anew (`ChunkGenerations`). At the start the build of every chunk stays for ten frames (`StartupSurface`), until the camera has its place, the chunks it wants take their pieces from it, and then the allocator is asked to give back what it can (`FMemory::Trim`). `UpdateChunkLod` measures from the view target, where the camera is in this frame, as the camera manager tells where it was in the frame before. A chunk without pieces draws its coarse mesh, which next to the pieces of a neighbor leaves gaps where the two do not meet, so a jump of the camera (`hex.PlanetSetCamera`) waits until every chunk that draws pieces from where it goes has them (`AHexPlanet::ArePiecesReadyAt`, `SetPiecePrefetch`, which builds those first, all at once, and keeps them; at most `JumpWaitMax` of `DA_HexPlanetCamera`): on Terra 61 chunks in about 130 ms in the background, while the old view goes on, and at the start at once from the build of the start. A chunk the camera still comes near without its pieces, nearer than the band, gets them built at once on the game thread (`BuildPiecesNow`, logged), which a fast orbit close up did not need. A jump is a cut (`AHexPlanetCameraPawn::SetPlacement`): the lazy sun stands where it should over the new place at once while it follows the camera, rather than gliding there over the dark side, and the camera manager drops what the renderer kept of the frames before (`SetGameCameraCutThisFrame`). `hex.PlanetStreamPieces 0` keeps the pieces of every chunk. `hex.Later <Seconds> <Command>` runs a console command later, through the player as the console does, so `-ExecCmds` can test a jump while the game runs, `HighResShot 1` included. Whatever stands on the ground of a cell reads its triangles from the build being shown, else builds them again on its own (`AHexPlanet::GetCellTriangles`, `HexPlanetSurface::BuildCellTriangles`, which the `Surface.Rebuild` and `Rivers` tests compare with a full build, as they do a build of the pieces alone), the objects and the bridges in parallel. `hex.PlanetMemory [Delay] [quit]` logs the triangles by level, what the surface holds on the GPU, the memory of the process and of the RHI and the ray tracing geometry of the chunks (`D3D12.DumpRayTracingGeometries`), with the times of five seconds of frames, Delay seconds after the planet is built; the time of the render thread is n/a while Slate draws a second window, such as the toast of `HighResShot`, for some seconds after it, as the engine then keeps only the slice between the presents of the two windows (`PresentWindow_RenderThread`), and `hex.PlanetRenderTimes [Frames]` logs the times the engine gives each frame with the windows shown. On Terra on 2026-11-08 at 1280x720, close to a range / from orbit, all this took the process from 9.7 / 10.3 GB committed and 6.3 / 6.6 GB in use to 8.7 / 8.4 and 5.4 / 5.4, its peak from 12.9 to 9.5 GB committed, the ray tracing geometry of the surface close up from 208 to 123 MB, its build from 486 to 308 ms and showing it from 571 to 220 ms, at the same frame times; about 2.5 GB of what the editor binary holds is memory its allocator, mimalloc, keeps for reuse (`FMallocUnused` with `-LLMCSV`), which a packaged game, with the binned allocator, should not keep. The debug views keep the switch: `hex.Wireframe` draws a copy of each chunk and of its coarse mesh, shown with them (`WireChunks`, `WireCoarseChunks`, made the first time, under the empty `Wireframe`), so in the band both show, and a coarse mesh counts as terrain for `hex.WireframeTarget`; as the surface keeps its meshes on the GPU alone, the copies come from a build of the surface shown when that view is turned on (`BuildWireSections`) and from each build shown after (`SetWireSection`). The `Surface.Rebuild` test compares the coarse sections too. It reads no UObject, so it runs on any thread, and builds the chunks in parallel (`ParallelFor`); the kit is read once (`LoadKit`, `SurfaceKit`). `AHexPlanet::SetCellLevels` changes the levels at once and rebuilds what they reach in `UE::Tasks`: `FindAffectedChunks` takes the cells whose level or massif step changed (`FindMassifSteps` over the whole planet), their neighbors, whose meshes change, and the neighbors of those, whose normals on the shared sides change, and rebuilds every chunk holding any of them, both sections. The normals on the sides towards a chunk that is not rebuilt sum those of its cells next to it, which are built again only for that (the halo), so no seam shows; the `Surface.Rebuild` test compares a partial rebuild with a full one. `MeanPeak` stays at the value of the first build, so the grid fade does not jump. The sea field and the water grid are built again in the same task when a cell crossed the coast or moved between shallows and deep sea, and `Tick` shows the result (`FinishRebuild`), starts the next rebuild if levels changed meanwhile, as calls during a rebuild are merged, and broadcasts `OnLevelsRebuilt`; `GetShownLevels` gives the levels of the surface shown. The sea field is a new texture each time with a new name (`MakeUniqueObjectName`), as one made under the name of a texture still there replaces that object in place and the water turns light turquoise. `hex.PlanetSetLevel <Cell> <Level> [Rings] [ToCell]` sets a cell and the cells up to `Rings` around it, or a line of cells along the arc to `ToCell` and `Rings` around it, for trying long chains (`hex.PlanetFocus` logs the latitude and longitude of its cell, for `hex.PlanetSetCamera`), and the log gives each rebuild's chunks and times: on Terra a stroke of a few cells rebuilds two or three chunks in about 6 ms in the background, plus about 70 ms for the sea field and water grid when the coast changes, and shows them in about 30 ms with the sea, and the first build of all chunks takes about 160 ms after about 850 ms of reading the kit.
- The levels and biomes are painted with a brush (`UHexPlanetPainter`, owned by `AHexPlanetPlayerController`): B (`PaintModeAction`) turns it on with its palette `SHexPlanetPalette` at the left, whose buttons never take the keyboard, 1 to 5 (`PaintLevelAction`, one action whose keys give their number through a scalar modifier) choose deep sea to mountains and turn it on, the biome buttons of the palette paint biomes instead, at once and only where the level of a cell allows them, X (`BrushSizeAction`) makes the brush one to three rings of cells, in a cycle (the size buttons of the palette are lit only while no tool or one that uses it is chosen, `UHexPlanetPainter::UsesBrushSize`, as Entrance, Carve, River and Bridge take no size and outline one cell, `GetOutlineSize`), and Ctrl+Z and Ctrl+Y (`UndoAction`, `RedoAction`) undo and redo whole strokes, up to `PaintUndoSteps`. Keys of symbols such as [ and ] are not used, as Unreal names them by the character of the layout, so on another layout they are other keys. While it is on, the left button paints instead of dragging the planet (`AHexPlanetCameraPawn::OnDragStarted`), and a stroke walks the arc between the cells of two frames, so a fast mouse leaves no gaps; a pentagon painted a mountain becomes hills. The cells of a stroke show at once as flat tiles of the color of their new level in a mesh on the planet (`PaintPreview`, `PaintLift`, `PaintSeaLift` over the sea, and `PaintMountainTop` as the top of a mountain, so a tile covers one), and on release the stroke goes to `SetCellLevels`; a tile goes once `GetShownLevels` has its level. Painting marks the levels as edited (`MarkLevelsEdited`), the overlay warns "Levels not saved", and undoing or redoing back to the step of the last save makes the planet saved again: each step has an `Id`, a layer's top is the `Id` of its top step or `BaseId`, the last step the cap of `PaintUndoSteps` dropped, and the save keeps the tops (`SavedTop`, `OnSaved`); every change a step makes passes `bByStep` (`MarkLevelsEdited`, `SetReliefOverrides`, `OnRiversChanged`, `UHexPlanetRivers::SetEdges`, `SetEntrances`), and any other change, such as `hex.PlanetSetLevel`, sets `bEditedOutsideSteps`, which only a save clears (`UHexPlanetPainter::UpdateSavedState`, `AHexPlanet::ClearEditedBySteps`), and `SaveViewAction` writes `levels.txt` and `biomes.txt` of a saved planet (`AHexPlanet::SaveLevels`), while `SaveAs` writes them for a new planet that was painted. A stroke of levels keeps in its step of Undo the biomes it changed. `create_input_assets.py` adds the lines of these actions to an existing `DA_HexKeyLegend` that lacks them (`ADDED_LEGEND_ACTIONS`, also the line of `SunFollowAction`).
- Each cell is a pair of a level and a biome, as on the flat map: the biomes with a `PlanetLetter` (`AHexPlanet::BiomeConfigs`, by letter: W water, S shoals, I ice, G grass, D sand, R rock, N snow, M swamp, T tundra, P steppe, from `create_biome_assets.py`; there is no forest biome, as forests are objects (`UHexPlanetObjects`, below) that may stand on any biome, even snow, and the letter F of older planets became G), and a cell holds an index into them (`CellBiomes`). Without `biomes.txt`, or when it is not valid, `HexPlanetGenerator::GenerateBiomes` places them by a climate, with all its values in `Biomes` of `DA_HexPlanet`: a temperature, 1 at the equator and 0 at the poles, colder by `HeightCooling` a level and moved by noise (`ClimateNoiseScale`, on the unit sphere), and a moisture of land, 1 at the coast falling over `MoistureReach` cells, drier in belts around `DryBeltTemperature`. The sea freezes below `IceTemperature`, and up to `ShoreIceRings` cells of sea from snow land freeze too as shore ice (`HexPlanetGenerator::AddShoreIce`), so a snowy coast never meets open water; `hex.PlanetShoreIce [Rings] [save]` does the same on a planet that has its biomes, and froze 9 cells of Islet and 2 of Terra; land is snow, tundra, desert, steppe, swamp (only plains) or grass by thresholds, and dry mountains of grass or steppe are rock. `Temperature` and `Moisture` of `FHexPlanetGeneration` (and the new planet panel) move them for a planet. A biome its level does not allow is replaced by the one most neighbors have that it allows, else the default of the level (`FindFittingBiome`), also when a level is painted. `M_HexPlanet` reads them from `CellBiomeTexture`, two pixels per cell at twice the column of `CellMarks`: the terrain and rock layers of the biome, in blue which of the cell and its neighbors are mountains and in alpha which are under the sea, and the terrain layers of the six neighbors, two to a byte (`WriteBiomePixels`); a mountain draws the rock layer of its biome as its terrain, down to its foot and the water, so a change of biomes uploads that texture and rebuilds no mesh; only ice that freezes or thaws rebuilds the sea field, in the rebuild task of levels. `BIOME_CODE` in `create_planet_assets.py` samples the layers of `TA_Biome_Color` and `TA_Biome_Normal` from the three planes of the world (`BiomeTileSize`, `TriplanarSharpness`, by the normal of the vertex, skipping planes of little weight), blends a cell into the neighbors across every side whose blend reaches a pixel, up to three other layers, half and half on the side and none `BiomeBlendWidth` inside, the line moved by a noise of two sizes (`BiomeBlendNoise`, `BiomeBlendDetail`) both cells see with opposite signs by the order of their layers, so they agree, and into the neighbor of a mountain that is not one by `MountainBlendShift`, and of land into its neighbor under the sea by `CoastBlendShift`, so where the winding coast lifts part of a cell of the sea above the water it draws the land, and a snowy coast shows no sand of the seabed, never past the apothem: of each pair of opposite sides one is at least that far, so no blend stops on a straight line where another side would take over, which taking only the nearest side and the other side at its corner did; the alpha of the layers and a noise of each layer (`BiomeEdgeNoise`) then decide which covers the other (`BiomeHeightBlend`, `BiomeBlendDepth`), so edges run in patches. The grass layer (`GrassTintLayer`) dries a little towards `GrassDryColor`, keeping its texture by its alpha: more the higher a hill (`GrassDryStart` to `GrassDryEnd`, `GrassHillDry`), in streaks of a bent noise on flat hill tops (`GrassStreak*`) and in rare patches anywhere (`GrassPatch*`), so wide grass is not one tone. Over that come the beach and the rock, each a property of the terrain layer (`BeachLayers0`..`2`, `RockLayers0`..`2`, which `AHexPlanet::SetBiomeLayers` takes from the first biome with that layer, `PlanetBeachLayer` and `MountainRockLayer`, and a rock of a mountain for itself), so they blend between cells as the layers do (`BlendMapped`): the beach below `BeachHeight` and on the slopes of the coast up to `ShoreSlopeTop`, in place of the rock there, sand for grass, swamp, tundra and steppe, snow for snow, the rock for a mountain, which from `SeabedHeight`, at the water, down over `SeabedFade` turns into the seabed of the layer, the heights of both deciding where each lies (`SeabedHeightBlend`, `SeabedBlendDepth`) and the upper part dusted with the beach (`SeabedDusting`), so a snowy shore goes through snowy sand into sand under the water (`SeabedLayers0`..`2`, from `PlanetSeabedLayer` of the biomes, else the beach), sand for snow, so snow never lies under the sea and the bottom is sand under every coast; the ice biome has the terrain of sand too, so the bottom does not change where the sea freezes; the rock on slopes (`SlopeStart`, `SlopeEnd`, steep enough that the gentle sides of hills keep their biome), snowed over on snow ground but for its high edges (`SnowRockCover`, `SnowRockPeek`), so a steep hill of snow stays snowy, snow above `SnowHeight` on flat ground, and the seabed darkens to `SeabedDarkening` at `DeepDepth`; the normal is in world space (`OutNormal`). Each layer is drawn at its own `TileSize`, `Tint`, `NormalStrength`, `HeightContrast` and `HeightOffset` of `DA_HexTerrainLayers`, which `AHexPlanet` gives the surface materials as `LayerParams` (`UHexTerrainLayersConfig::CreateParamsTexture`, a column per layer; its black default keeps `BiomeTileSize`), and a rock layer with a `ScreeLayer` or `GravelLayer` there is drawn as three (`FBiomeLayers::Sample`): its cliff where the ground is steeper than `RockCliffFlatStart` to `RockCliffFlatEnd`, its gravel where it is flatter than `RockGravelFlatStart` to `RockGravelFlatEnd`, the scree between them and in gullies (`RockGullyScree`), the flatness moved by a noise (`RockSetNoise`, `RockSetNoiseScale`) and the height of each deciding where they meet (`RockSetHeightBlend`, `RockSetBlendDepth`), so a mountain drawn as the rock of its biome has cliffs, scree and gravel at its foot without another table. Its GPU cost is not measured yet. The layers Tundra and Steppe were added to `Tools/HexTextures`. - Rivers run along the sides of the cells, from corner to corner (`UHexPlanetRivers`, a component of `AHexPlanet`, and `HexPlanetRivers`), so for movement a river lies between two cells: a move across it costs `RiverCrossCost` of `DA_HexPlanet` times more unless a bridge stands on that side (`GetCrossingFactor`, shown in `SHexHoverInfo`; nothing moves yet). A side may hold one only between plains and hills, so a side whose cell becomes sea or a mountain drops out until it is painted back, and a save leaves it out. `rivers.json` of a planet holds the sides, a line each with its two cells and whether a bridge stands there (`FHexRiverEdge`, `HexPlanetFile::SaveRivers`), so Git merges it; a planet without it generates them and writes it at once, even empty (`HexPlanetRivers::Generate`, `River*` and `BridgeSpacing` of `DA_HexPlanet`): each starts at a corner at the foot of mountains, else of hills, at least `RiverSourceSeaDistance` rings inland, and walks along sides between two cells of one level, never higher, to the corner a side takes nearest the nearest cell of the sea (carried out along the rings and checked against the neighbors, so the distance changes smoothly and a river runs straight towards the coast rather than wandering over rings of equal distance), up to `RiverFlatSteps` in a row that bring it little nearer; at each corner it turns left or right, and turning the same way at two corners in a row, which takes it off to the side, costs `RiverStraightness` against how much nearer a side takes it, with `RiverWander` of randomness (the `Rivers` test wants fewer than 40% of the sides turning it the same way at both ends; 20 of 92 there). It ends at a corner on the coast or another river, kept from running next to the rivers before it, and a bridge goes about every `BridgeSpacing` sides (Terra 566 sides with 56 bridges, Islet 38 with 3). Which way a side flows is not stored but found from the net (`BuildNet`): towards the nearest corner on the coast along the rivers, else towards the lowest corner of a net with none, as bits of each cell (the sides in the low byte, in the high byte those that flow from corner K - 1 to K). The water does not follow the sides but one smooth line, the same for every cell, so the water, its banks and its valley join across the sides without steps (`HexPlanetRivers::BuildCurve`, `FCurve`). The net becomes lines: at a corner of two rivers a line goes on through whichever way they flow, so a river stays one line where its flow parts or meets (a split corner is no spring), and at a corner of three the river flowing out and the largest coming in (most sides upstream) go on while the third ends there, moved onto the line of the others, the point of it nearest that corner, so the waters join; each line runs downstream where most of its sides flow, and `FlowSigns` says where the flow runs back. A line goes through the corner it starts at, the middles of its sides and the corner it ends at; how straight it runs, and so how far it leaves the sides of the cells, is `RiverCornerCut` (each corner between two sides pulled that share of the way onto the chord between their middles: 0 along the sides, 1, the default of the class, straight through their middles, where a zigzag of sides becomes a straight line a quarter of a cell from its corners; `DA_HexPlanet` has 0.6), `RiverRelax` (passes that straighten it further over several sides) and `RiverMaxOffset` (how far it may leave its sides at most, 0 for no limit, held before and after it is rounded off); the `Rivers` test measures them (about 4 along the sides, 22 through the middles, 8 with a limit of 8, 45 with six passes). Then it is rounded off `RiverSmoothing` times (Chaikin), wound by `RiverMeander` of a noise of the place (`RiverMeanderScale`), fading in over `RiverMeanderFade` of a cell from the ends of a line, and a river that ends on the coast goes on `RiverMouthLength` into the sea (`FCurve::Mouths`); a spring, a corner of one river away from the sea, narrows it by `Narrowing` over `RiverSourceTaper` of a cell. `hex.PlanetRiverShape [CornerCut] [Relax] [MaxOffset]` tries the shape while the game runs (`AHexPlanet::SetRiverShape`, which builds every chunk again, or the first build when it comes from `-ExecCmds`), and without arguments goes back to `DA_HexPlanet`. Each segment is listed for the cells within `GetReach` of it (`CellSegments`, two rings around the cell of its middle), so two cells that share a point find the same nearest point (`FindNearest`, which the `Rivers` test checks on every side). `HexPlanetSurface::Build` takes that line and lowers the ground into a valley along it (`RiverValleyDepth`, a flat bottom `RiverValleyBottom` wide each way from the line and slopes of `RiverValleySlope`), so a river lies a little below its cells, and the rebuild of changed levels rebuilds the cells whose bits changed and their rings (`FindChangedCells`). `M_HexPlanet` (`RIVER_CODE` in `create_planet_assets.py`) finds the exact distance to the line for each pixel, as distances in the vertices, blended by triangles about 17 across on flat plains, missed a line that crossed one, and their sides, which do not fit between two lines or past the end of one, drew false water: `UHexPlanetRivers::UpdateTexture` lists for each sector of each cell (the triangle from its center to corners K - 1 and K, which the angle of the fourth UV gives) the segments of the line, every other point of it, within reach of anything of a river there (water, banks, the gap from the run-up, plus 25 for the pieces whose middle moves), and `CellRivers`, a pixel per sector at six times the column of `CellMarks`, gives the first of them in RGB and their number in alpha, while `RiverSegments`, a float texture 4096 wide, holds two texels a segment: its ends as directions from the center with the narrowing at each, and in the first whether it is frozen (16, either cell of its side has a biome of `RiverFrozenBiomes`) and flows from its first end to its second (32). The nearest gives the distance, the flow, which way is away from the river for its banks and which side of it a pixel lies on for its lanes; a change of biomes writes them again (`RefreshTexture`), and a new planet or more segments make the textures anew (`AHexPlanet::ApplyRiverTextures`). The water is `RiverWidth` wide and at least about a pixel, and lets its bed show through as the shallows of the sea do: the sand of `RiverBedLayer` (layer 1) tinted by `RiverBedTint`, under `RiverWaterEdge` to `RiverWaterMiddle` of the colors of `RiverShallowColor` to `RiverDeepColor`, darker under its banks (`RiverEdgeShade`), with faint ripples carried downstream (`RiverFlowSpeed`, `RiverRipple*`) and, so it shows that it flows, lines in lanes along the river that drift downstream, broken into dashes of a noise carried with the water in the same two phases (`RiverStreak*`); on steep ground denser and brighter lines are the foam of rapids (`RiverRapids*`). It is rough enough that the sun makes no glints on it (`RiverRoughness` 0.65). Its banks are wet earth (`RiverBank*`, the edge moved by `RiverEdgeNoise`) with a volume alike on both sides whatever the light, darker at the foot and lighter on the crest (`RiverBankShade`, `RiverBankHighlight`), and a normal bent a little across them (`RiverBankRelief` over `RiverLeveeWidth`, away from the nearest point of the line); a frozen river is still, cracked ice (`RiverIce*`). Below the sea it fades out over `RiverMouthDepth`, so at its mouth the water of the sea meets the bed alone, and within `RiverMouthCalm` of the coast, by the distance of the sea field, it grows as light as `RiverShallowColor` and its rapids calm down, so it joins the shallow water it meets; where a frozen river reaches the sea its ice goes on as `RiverMouthFloes` broken floes, smaller further out to `RiverMouthFloeReach`, the first on the edge of the water (`AHexPlanet::UpdateFloes`, before the pack, which keeps away from them). Where an open river reaches the sea its water pours out into it: `AHexPlanet::UpdateRiverMouths` gives `M_HexPlanetWater` the mouths nearest the point under the camera, up to 8 (`RiverMouth0..7` with alpha 1 when given, `RiverMouthDir0..7`, `RIVER_MOUTHS` of the script), and `PLUME_CODE` draws from each a fan `RiverPlumeWidth` wide at the mouth that spreads by `RiverPlumeSpread` out to `RiverPlumeLength`, starting `RiverPlumeBack` inland so it covers the edge of the water there: the foam of the shore and the breakers give way in it (`RiverPlumeCalm`), so no line of foam lies across a mouth, its water is silty (`RiverPlumeColor`, `RiverPlumeTint`) and rougher (`RiverPlumeChop`), lines in `RiverPlumeLanes` lanes stream out with the river and wander as they spread (`RiverPlumeLineWidth`, `RiverPlumeDashLength`, `RiverPlumeSpeed`, `RiverPlumeFoam`, `RiverPlumeWobble`), and froth churns where it meets the sea (`RiverPlumeFroth`); all of them are values of `DA_HexPlanetWater`. It reflects by `RiverRoughness` (the custom node gives the roughness), hides the grid line of its side, and comes after `CANOPY_CODE`, so a forest never covers it; objects keep `RiverWidth` / 2 + `RiverMeander` + `RiverBankWidth` from its line (`GetRiverDistance`), and boulders stay out of the valleys. - `AHexPlanet` gives each cell a level from levels.txt or `HexPlanetGenerator`, and builds two mesh sections per face, the second with the mountains, and keeps where the vertices and triangles of each cell lie in them (`CellMeshes`). Frequency 80 is as many cells as 256 x 256. The first UV is 0 in the center of a cell and 1 at its sides, which `M_HexPlanet` turns into the grid line (`GridWidth`, never under about a pixel, `GridMinPixels`, nor over `GridMaxPixels` for the half each cell draws, so a slope facing the camera does not draw it twice as wide as flat ground seen at a slant), and its V is the height above the sea as a share of the mean peak of mountain cells, by which the line fades out from `GridFadeStart` to `GridFadeEnd`, so it does not show on mountains, and below the sea over `GridSeaFade`, as the lines of the cells of the sea lie on the water: `WaterGrid` is a strip inside the sides of each such cell at the height of the sea (`AHexPlanet::BuildWaterGrid`, as wide as the widest line, so change it with `GRID_LINE_CODE`), whose U runs as on the surface, and `M_HexPlanetWaterGrid` draws the same line there as translucent black (`WaterGridDarkness`), after the water and before the clouds by sort priority, and hides it with the water of the cells `hex.Water` hides; the second is the UV of the piece. The surface draws no vertex colors of its own, and the tiles of painting draw colors of their own (`PreviewTile`, `AHexPlanet::GetPreviewMaterial`, which has `CellMarks` and the colors of the outlines, and whose tiles carry the third and fourth UVs of their cells, so the edge of the brush shows on them); the surface draws the biomes, see below. Clouds are four octaves of 3D value noise of the direction from the center, computed in `CLOUD_CODE` without textures, so they never repeat, the large octave shifting the finer ones so their edges curl into puffs; they turn around the axis of the planet, and each octave moves along the radius and back at its own pace (`CloudMorph`, `CloudMorphSpeed`), which takes another slice of the noise, so they grow, part and dissolve in place. The two finest octaves fade out where a pixel covers more than `CloudDetailFade` of their cells, as from far away they only draw streaks. All their values are in `DA_HexPlanetClouds` (`UHexPlanetCloudConfig`, created by the script only when missing, so tune it by hand), and `AHexPlanet::SetCloudParameters` gives every float and color of it, with `PlanetRadius`, to each material that draws the clouds or their shadows as parameters of the same names, once when it is made, so a new value needs only the property and the parameter (`CLOUD_PARAMETERS`, `SHADOW_PARAMETERS`, `SHELL_PARAMETERS` in the script, whose defaults come from the class). `AHexPlanet` draws them as a sphere (`Clouds`, `CloudRings`) at `CloudHeight` above the sea with the unlit translucent `M_HexPlanetClouds`, sorted after the water, as both have their bounds around the center. They fill `CloudThickness` around their height: `SHELL_LIFT_CODE` puts the shell on the side of it towards the camera, and `CLOUDS_CODE` marches the view through it from there, forwards where it goes in and backwards from the far side of the shell, which past the rim of the planet is where a view comes out of its second pass, with more looks the longer the view is in it (`CloudVolumeStep`, up to `CloudVolumeSteps`, offset by frame for the temporal filter); a point needs more cloud at the top and the bottom (`CloudVolumeRound`), so big clouds fill it and small ones stay thin, its bottom is darker (`CloudVolumeBaseShade`), and the light it takes grows with the length (`CloudVolumeDensity`), so along the shell at the horizon they stand up as heaps. From above it takes about two looks, and at 1280x720 on the machine it was made on all of it cost about 0.2 ms of GPU over a single look. They also look puffy from above: the density is a height, and a second look `CloudLightStep` of a cloud towards the sun puts a side where it rises there in a cool grey shade (`CloudRelief`, `CloudShadeColor`), only where there is cloud. They thin out at night and where the shell is seen edge on, so the rim of the planet shows no stripes, have `CloudOpacity` from the farthest camera, thin to `CloudNearOpacity` as the distance from the camera to a cloud, plus `CloudHeight`, comes down from `CloudFarHeight` to `CloudFadeEnd`, so close above them the land shows through, and fade out from there to `CloudFadeStart`, so the clouds next to the camera are gone while those far off stay. The camera at its closest is at about the height of the clouds, where a flat shell only shows streaks, so `AHexPlanet` draws the shell twice: `Clouds` below the camera, and `SkyClouds`, whose material has `CloudSkyLayer` 1 and which `SHELL_LIFT_CODE` moves out to `CloudSkyHeight`, above the close camera, as a sky whose clouds show over the horizon, with bounds widened for it. As the camera comes down through `CloudSkySwitch` above the sea the one below fades out and the sky fades in over `CloudSkySwitchFade` on both sides, so there are always clouds, and `AHexPlanet::Tick` hides the shell that is faded out. The material is two sided, and the side away from the camera fades as the shell seen edge on does, so from above the far half is hidden and from under the sky its inside shows, which fades less edge on (`CloudEdgeOnFadeNear`). A cloud whose line from the camera dips below the sea is hidden, as the water writes no depth and the cloud, drawn after it, would show through it near the horizon. Their shadows (`CLOUD_SHADOW_CODE`, `CloudShadowStrength`) darken the base color of `M_HexPlanet`, so only the lit side, and the color of `M_HexPlanetWater` with its glints, by the cloud where the ray to the sun meets the shell, so they lie under their clouds; `AHexPlanet` gives all three `PlanetRadius`, `CloudHeight` and `SunDirection`.
- Relief comes from a kit of meshes, `DA_HexPlanetKit` (`UHexPlanetKitConfig`), one kit per level. A cell takes the piece of its level for the mask of neighbors on the same level, chosen and turned by `HexMeshBuilder::GetJoinedPiece`, as the mountains of the flat map, and a version and a symmetric turn by a hash of the cell. `HexPlanetKit::PlaceVertex` bends each of the six triangles of the regular hex onto the triangle from the center of the cell to two of its corners by the same weights, so direction D of a piece is neighbor D and its corner D is corner D - 1 of the cell. Two levels share the slope between them, so the side of a hex lies halfway down it and its grid line with it, and a coast runs near the sides: a corner lies at the mean top of its three cells, and a side at `GetSideHeight`, the mean of the tops of its two cells in its middle, bending linearly to its corners, which both cells find alike. Height 0 of a piece goes onto the top of the cell and its edge, `-FootDepth`, onto the line of the side, so the higher cell falls to it and the lower one rises, and heights between blend them over `FadeHeight` above the edge. Land bends its top onto the line near a side to the same level (`bTopFollowsCorners`), so with `FadeHeight` equal to `FootDepth` a side that a piece models anywhere between 0 and the edge lands on its neighbor, and a point on a side towards another level, or at a corner of such a side, goes onto the line whatever its height; the `Seams` test checks it. A piece must therefore lie at the edge on a side towards another level, steepest there so the two halves join smoothly, within the edge and 0 on other sides, and have a vertex in the middle of every side.
- Neighboring mountains and hills differ, and chains rise and fall, as the map is meant to be a maze of ranges: each kit has `Variety` values in `FHexPlanetKit`, which `import_planet_kit.py` sets from `VARIETY`. A cell may take its piece mirrored (`bMirror`, by a hash: sector D goes to direction 5 - D and its corners swap, looked up by the mirrored mask, `MirrorMask` in `HexPlanetSurface.cpp`, also in the coarse mesh). Each cell stretches its piece above `ScaleBase` by a factor (`HexPlanetSurface::FindRelief`, `CellRelief`, shown in `SHexHoverInfo`): from `HeightScaleMin` to `HeightScaleMax` by a noise of the place of about `HeightNoiseCells` cells, so a chain is low for a while, then high, plus `HeightJitter` by cell and, with `PeakChance` that grows where the noise is high, `PeakScale` more for a big peak. It depends on the place and the level of a cell alone, so painting changes it only with the level and `FindAffectedChunks` needs nothing more. `HexPlanetKit::BuildFrame` blends it as the massif, the mean of two cells in the middle of a side and of three at a corner, so a shared side gets the same factor from both cells; land fades it out where its top follows the side lines (`bTopFollowsCorners`), as it lies on them anyway. The center of each piece also moves by up to `CenterShift` of the radius in a direction of its own (`FCellFrame::CenterShift`, added by the weight of the center, which is 0 on the sides), so peaks stand off the middles and the cells of a chain do not line up. The inside of each cell, fading out before its sides (`Inside` in `PlaceVertex`), also takes a shape of its own (`Shape` of `FHexPlanetKit`): its height above `ScaleBase` as a share of `ShapeHeight` is raised to a power between `SharpnessMin` and `SharpnessMax` by a hash (`FCellFrame::Sharpness`), so one peak is a blunt dome and the next a needle, a noise of the place adds summits and notches (`CragHeight`, `CragScale`), and the inside turns around the center by up to `Twist` degrees (`FCellFrame::Twist`), so ridges do not all point alike. Neighboring peaks join into a chain: the ground rises by `RidgeLift` along the line from the center of a cell to the middle of each side towards the same level, from nothing at the center to all of it on the side, `RidgeWidth` of the side wide, so a saddle between two mountains lies high and a range reads as one chain with peaks on it rather than a row of teeth; a side of a narrow chain (a cell with at most two neighbors on its level) takes all of it, while inside a massif only `RidgeMassifShare` of the sides do, chosen by a hash of the pair of cells, and the rest `RidgeMassifLow` (`FCellFrame::RidgeWeights`), so a massif is no net of ridges. A chain one cell wide still looks massive (`Bulk` of `FHexPlanetKit`): the low ground of a piece, which the blend into the side line would flatten into a strip of low land along the sides, rises by `BulkLift` (times the root of the factor of the height), from nothing at the foot to all of it `BulkReach` of the way up to the peak, so the rock reaches out to the sides on a full base; it depends on the height on the piece alone, so a side shared with the same level gets it alike and the foot stays on the side lines. Where a noise along the ranges says so (`SplitNoiseCells`, `SplitThreshold`, `FCellFrame::Split`), the crest of a cell with neighbors on its level splits into two blades with a couloir between them along the lines from its center to the middles of those sides, `SplitDepth` deep and `SplitBlade` high at `SplitWidth` of a cell radius, only inside, so it joins again on the shared sides. They move no vertex in or out, so they cost no triangles and no measurable GPU. High ground breaks into sharp faces like split stone: a ridged noise of the place moves it by `JaggedHeight` (`JaggedScale`), by a share that grows from `FacetHeightStart` to `FacetHeightEnd` above the sea (`HexPlanetKit::GetFacetShare`), so small mountains stay smooth, and the same share goes into X of the second UV, by which `M_HexPlanet` shades that ground flat by triangle from `PosDdx` and `PosDdy`, each face leaning at random by regions of `FacetCell` (`FacetStrength`, `FacetTilt` of `DA_HexPlanet`); ray tracing has no such change across a pixel and keeps the vertex normal. The sides are rock down to the foot too (`Rock` of `FHexPlanetKit`): gullies cut down the fall line by `GullyHeight`, channels of a ridged noise of the angle around the center of the cell and, stretched, of the distance from it (`GullyCount`, `GullyLength`), most on the middle of the slopes and fading before the sides, so spurs stand between them; the share of a gully at a vertex goes into Y of the second UV, by which `M_HexPlanet` darkens it (`CreviceDarkening`) and lets snow reach `GullySnowReach` further down it on steeper ground (`GullySnowFlatness`), so snow lies in streaks; and ground with faces steps in ledges of `LedgeStep` (`LedgeStrength`, a function of the height and the place). On Terra the planet has about 10 million triangles, close to a range about 3.1 million drawn where 2.7 were, and on the machine it was made on that cost about 0.16 ms of GPU there, 0.03 ms for the whole planet, and about 0.8 GB more memory of the process. Finer ravines than a lattice step (about 8 units) would need a rock normal stretched along the slope in the material. Boulders lie at the foot of the mountains on the edge of ranges as instances apart from the surface (`FHexPlanetBoulders` in `Boulders` of `DA_HexPlanetKit`): `HexPlanetSurface::Build` puts up to `MaxPerSide` on each side of a cell of mountains towards land or shallows (`MinLevel`), along it and up to `Spread` into the neighbor, on the ground a flat tile of the neighbor would have there, sunk by `Sink` of their size, leaning up to `Tilt` (`FBuild::Boulders`, by chunk, so a rebuild replaces those of its chunks), and `AHexPlanet` draws them with a `UHierarchicalInstancedStaticMeshComponent` per mesh (`BoulderComponents`, `UpdateBoulders`, culled past `CullDistance`), with the rock layer of the biome of their mountain as the first custom data of an instance, which `M_HexPlanetBoulder` of `create_planet_assets.py` projects from the three planes of the world, lighter or darker by `BoulderTint`, and a change of biomes updates. The four rocks come from `generate_planet_kit.py` (`Boulder`, an icosphere cut by planes into broad faces, a normal per face) and `import_planet_kit.py` puts them and the material into the kit, so run `create_planet_assets.py` before it. Terra has about 13,000 of them, about 0.3 million triangles close to a range, and on the machine it was made on the shapes, faces and boulders cost about 0.03 ms of GPU for the whole planet and nothing measurable close to mountains. The snow line stays at `SnowHeight`, so only the high peaks get snow. A mountain never takes a factor below `HeightScaleFloor`, so a low one still reads as a mountain, and from `HighRockStart` to `HighRockEnd` above the sea `M_HexPlanet` covers the ground with the rock of the biome whatever its slope, so it is rock and not grass while hills stay below it. A lone cell (at most `LoneNeighbors` neighbors on its level) with no neighbor on its level takes at least `LoneHeightMin` and raises its height from the foot to the power `LoneSharpness` (`FCellFrame::LoneDome`), while a spur, the end of a chain with one such neighbor, skips both, as on the piece of a spur, whose peak stands off its center, they made a leaning tower, takes `SpurHeightScale` of its factor of the height (not below `HeightScaleFloor`), so a chain steps down at its end, and is never sharper than a cone; both stand on a dome over its whole cell of `LoneBase` of its peak (`FCellFrame::LoneBase`, on the height and not on the piece, as the low heights of a piece blend into the side line, and only inside, as such a cell may share a side with one neighbor) and moves its center only `LoneShift` of `CenterShift`, so it fills its cell instead of standing as a thin spike. A cell with no neighbors on its level also has `LoneSummitStyles` more versions besides its pieces (`FCellFrame::Summits`, the styles in `BuildCell`): two summits of unlike heights, three in an uneven cluster, and a main peak with a lower shoulder, turned by a hash, each a peak of `SummitHeight` and `SummitWidth` over its first piece lowered to `SummitBase` of its height above the side lines, only inside; the factor of the height counts by its root there, so a high cell does not make a tower. The hash and the Version tool choose among them and the pieces. `FBuild::UnmatchedRidgePoints` counts the points on sides between mountains (a kit without `bTopFollowsCorners`) that only one cell built, and the `Surface.Rebuild` test wants none, mirrored pieces included; the `Seams` test gives every cell a random factor and land a plateau. Hills have four styles of bumps, one per version (`HILL_STYLES`: knolls, a ridge, two big knolls, many small), kept gentle enough that the rock of slopes (`SlopeEnd` of `DA_HexPlanet`, 0.8) does not ring them like craters. They fade out before every side, so a group of hills drew a mound in each cell with flat ground along the sides; so hills with neighbors on their level rise together into an upland (`PlateauLift` and `PlateauFill` of `FHexPlanetKit`, 16 and 0.45 from `VARIETY` of `import_planet_kit.py`, in `HexPlanetKit::PlaceVertex`): the ground rises by `PlateauLift` times the factor of the height, on the sides between two such cells, at the corners of three and in the middle, by the share of the sides of a cell towards neighbors on its level (`FCellFrame::PlateauCenter`, `PlateauSides`, `PlateauCorners`: the mean of the cells of a side or corner that all share the plateau, else 0, and never a pentagon), blended as the massif and eased in from a side towards another level, so the inside of a big group rises by all of it, its edge by about half, a chain by a quarter and a pair hardly, and it ramps down to the side lines. The bumps take only part of the lift, so the ground between them comes up towards their tops: at a `PlateauFill` of 1 a bump twice as high as the lift keeps its top, and the ground never folds. On Terra it adds no triangles and no time to the build; the steepest edges of an upland show a little rock of slopes, and its highest knolls can reach `HighRockStart`. Below the top of the plains the ground on slopes moves up and down by a noise of the place (`HexPlanetKit::ApplyWobble`: `CoastWobble`, `CoastWobbleScale`, `CoastWobbleDetail` of `DA_HexPlanet`), most halfway down a slope and not at all on the tops, clamped to the slope, so the coast and the edge of the shelf wind across the cells; it is a pure function of the point, so cells that share a point agree, and the slope pieces of the deep sea, shallows and plains have 6 lattice steps instead of 4 for it. On the machine it was made on at 1080p this cost about 0.06 ms of GPU close to mountains (2.0 to 2.4 million triangles) and 0.1 ms over a coast (2.9 to 3.3 million), nothing for the whole planet (3.2 ms, coarse meshes), and about 0.4 GB more memory of the process.
- `Tools/HexPlanetKit/generate_planet_kit.py` writes the land pieces (deep sea, shallows, plains, hills with bumps) as GLB files, which Blender opens, with `kit.json` to `Saved/HexPlanetKit`, and `import_planet_kit.py`, run with the commandlet, imports them to `Content/HexPlanet/Kit` and writes `DA_HexPlanetKit`, so change the scripts and not the asset. Mountains are the meshes of the flat map, with the planet's own lighter massif, sampled as a height field on a lattice of 12 steps (`Subdivisions`, as their 24 steps came to 30 million triangles; 8 were too coarse for gullies), and what they model past their cell at notches is left out, as the neighbor rises to the side there. The planet reads the vertices of every piece, so they need CPU access, which the import and `assign_hex_mountains.py` give; run the import again after rebuilding the mountains. The planet has about 10 million triangles, so `r.RayTracing.ResidentGeometryMemoryPoolSizeInMB` in `DefaultEngine.ini` is 2048: at the default 400 the new ray tracing geometry of a section whose render state was recreated was evicted at once, and `UProceduralMeshComponent`, which the surface used then, hit an ensure ("geometry 'Surface' is evicted"); `UHexPlanetMeshComponent` references an evicted geometry, so it comes back.
- The 12 pentagons are never mountains (a mountain there becomes hills) and stay passable, a flat tile of their level that slopes to the side lines towards other levels, built with the same frame as any cell whose kit has no piece; each is meant for a gameplay element that gives bonuses, not decided yet, and a set piece of its own may come later, as long as it can be walked over. From each rises an aurora (`AHexPlanet::BuildAuroras`, `Auroras`): a `UNiagaraComponent` of `NS_HexPlanetAurora` at the height of the sea over the pentagon, with Z away from the center of the planet, drawn after the clouds and left out of ray tracing (`SetVisibleInRayTracing`), as light added to the scene needs none there and its ribbons, moved by their material, hung the GPU in a play session in the editor (`DXGI_ERROR_DEVICE_HUNG`, a page fault in a ray tracing shader). The Niagara system is an asset for an artist to tune by hand, not made by a script (it was built once through the NiagaraToolsets of the editor's MCP server, which `HexHero.uproject` enables): `Curtain` and `CurtainOuter`, each a burst of particles on a ring that live for ever, drawn as a ribbon whose width stands up (`CustomSideVector`, facing Z), jittered out and in by a random velocity that drag stops, the outer wider, lower and bluer, and `RisingLight`, sprites aligned to their velocity that rise from a ring and sway in a curl noise. Its materials come from `create_planet_assets.py`: `M_HexPlanetAurora` for the ribbons (`AURORA_CODE`: rays of a value noise along the curtain that drift and run up, brightest at its lower edge, from `AuroraColorLow` to `AuroraColorHigh` as it rises, `AuroraFlipV` for which way V runs, broken into `AuroraDrapes` with dark gaps), whose world position offset (`AURORA_OFFSET_CODE`) folds the curtains in and out and sways them along themselves, more the higher up (`AuroraFold`, `AuroraFoldCount`, `AuroraFoldSpeed`, `AuroraSway`, out along the normal of the ribbon, as the object position a Niagara material reads is not that of its system and moved the curtains far off) and `M_HexPlanetAuroraSpark` for the sprites, both additive, unlit and two sided, with their values as material parameters, tinted by the particle color and its alpha, brighter at night, fading out next to the camera, a little brighter from far away (`AuroraFarBoost` from `AuroraFarStart` to `AuroraFarEnd` from the camera), so the 12 read as landmarks from orbit, and hidden where the line from the camera dips below the sea (`AURORA_COMMON_CODE`), as the water writes no depth and they would show through it. They read the sun and the radius of the planet from `MPC_HexPlanet`, which `AHexPlanet::Tick` sets, as Niagara draws with the materials themselves; `PlanetRadius` is 0 outside the game, which turns the test off, so the preview of Niagara shows them.
- `AHexPlanetCameraPawn` orbits it with the keys of `DA_HexInput`, and the sun follows the point it looks at unless `hex.PlanetSun` is 0 (`UpdateSun`): it shines from `SunAzimuth` of `DA_HexPlanetCamera`, degrees from the north of that point towards the right of a camera facing north, so turning the camera turns no shadow, at `SunCloseElevation` to `SunFarElevation` by the zoom, and it is lazy: it stands still, so shadows do not slide and virtual shadow maps keep their cache, until it lags `SunLagShare` of the distance of the camera (at most `SunLagMaxDegrees`) behind where it should stand and the camera has stood still for `SunSettleTime`, or lags `SunMaxLagFactor` times that while it moves, and then it glides there over `SunMoveTime` (each move is logged as Verbose of `LogHexHero`). L (`SunFollowAction`) toggles it; then the sun stays where it was, so turning the planet shows its night side and the shadows, and the panel of settings shows "Sun: Fixed" (`AHexPlanetCameraPawn::GetSunStatus`). `hex.PlanetFocus <Cell> [Distance]` glides to a cell, and at startup puts the camera there at once. `HighResShot` in `-ExecCmds` shoots on the first frames, before a glide ends.
- `AHexPlanetPlayerController` outlines the cell under the cursor in white (`OutlineColor`), which `AHexPlanet::FindCellAlongRay` finds by stepping a quarter of a cell along the ray from the sphere of the highest tops until it goes under the top of the cell there, the water for a cell of the sea, as its grid lines and outline lie on the water and a slanted ray to its bed reached cells further on, and halving the last step; it counts a point a hundredth of a unit above a top as under it, as the last step lies on the water and rounding alone lost the cell there for a frame, which made the panel at the cursor blink, and its neighbors in blue (`NeighborOutlineColor`) only while the debug wireframe draws them too (`AHexPlanet::SetHoveredCell`), whose own colors match (`WireHoverColor`, `WireNeighborColor`); while painting it outlines only the edge of the brush (`BrushOutlineColor`). An outline is no mesh of its own: it colors the grid lines on both sides of the edge of a set of cells, each half `MarkWidth` wide and at least `MarkMinPixels`, glowing by `MarkGlow`, so it is centered on the edge, lies on the real relief, and over the sea on the water; at a bend of the edge the half of a marked side goes on past its corner into the side next to it, as far as the band of that side, so the line joins with a sharp miter, as a disc around the corner left dots. `CellMarks` is a texture with a pixel per cell, 2048 to a row, whose red holds the sides to outline as bits in `MarkPrimaryColor` (`OutlineColor` or `BrushOutlineColor`, which `SetHoveredCell` gives the materials), and green those in `NeighborOutlineColor` (`AHexPlanet::SetCellMarks`, which uploads it when it changes), and every vertex of the surface and of `WaterGrid` has the pixel of its cell as its third UV (`HexPlanetSurface::GetCellUV`, the column and row apart, as UVs are half floats that hold whole numbers exactly only up to 2048) and where it lies in a regular cell whose side K faces K turns of a side as its fourth (`GetLocalUV`), so `MARK_CODE` in `create_planet_assets.py` finds the side nearest a pixel and the other side at its nearest corner by its angle, in ints, as fmod and exp2 of floats dropped some sides to the one before. Below the sea the surface hides it at once and `M_HexPlanetWaterGrid` draws it, so the strips of the sea are as wide as `MarkWidth` too, except in the cells whose water `hex.Water` hides, as their strip is hidden with it: `AHexPlanet::SetWaterHiddenCells` sets blue of `CellMarks` in them (every cell while all the water is hidden), and there the surface draws the grid lines and the outline on the seabed. A hole of more than one cell also gets its rim in alpha of `CellMarks`, bits of sides as in red, which only `M_HexPlanetWaterGrid` draws (`MARK_HOLE_BITS` in `MARK_CODE`), in `WaterHideOutlineColor`, so the water around the hole outlines it while the seabed keeps the outline of the cell alone. It shows the cell in `SHexHoverInfo`. Its `SHexDebugOverlay` shows the lines of `DA_HexKeyLegend` whose actions the pawn and the controller bind (`UHexKeyLegendConfig::GetBoundActions`, `GetSections` with `OnlyActions`), once the pawn has bound them, and so does the flat map (`AHexMapPlayerController::UpdateLegend`), so neither lists keys that do nothing there, such as U and L on the flat map; each line is logged as Verbose of `LogHexHero` (`LogSections`). The overlay has the same panels of settings as the flat map (`SHexDebugOverlay::AddViewStatus`, which both controllers use) and the red warning while the camera differs from the one in `LastView.json`, and `hex.HoverInfo` hides the panel at the cursor.
- `hex.CopyCamera` (Ctrl+C) on the planet copies `hex.LoadPlanet <Folder> | hex.PlanetSetCamera <Latitude> <Longitude> <Heading> <Distance>` (`FHexPlanetCameraPlacement`, heading clockwise from north), which brings the view back from any level and logs the cell under it; on the planet already open the camera jumps there once the pieces around that place are built (see the surface). `hex.LoadMap` also works from the planet, opening `Lvl_HexWorld` with that map. Ctrl+Shift and a number key (`SaveBookmarkAction`, `IA_HexSaveBookmark`) save the camera in a slot of the bookmarks of the planet or flat map open, and Ctrl and the key (`JumpBookmarkAction`) put it back there, 1 to 9 and 0, the tenth (`HexCameraBookmarks`, `Saved/CameraBookmarks.json`, a block per planet and per map, of the player and not in Git, as the developer chose on 2026-11-10): on the planet with its layer, as `hex.PlanetSetCamera` does, once the pieces around the place are built and through a trip to the other layer, on a planet not saved kept in memory until a save gives it a folder (`SaveView`), and on the flat map at once with its turn, zoom and mode; `hex.Bookmark <save|jump> <0-9>` does the same from the console.
- `LastView.json` (`FHexLastView`) holds a block per kind of view, so more can be added: `flatMap` (`FHexLastFlatMapView`, the map and its camera) and `planet` (`FHexLastPlanetView`, the folder of the planet and its camera, which counts as not saved on another planet), and `bPlanetOpen`, whether the planet was open last, written by `HexLastView::SetPlanetOpen` whenever either level opens. `HexLastView::Parse` also reads the flat layout of before the blocks. The planet camera is saved with `SaveViewAction` (Ctrl+S; Alt+S is Simulate of the editor, which a play session takes before the game). `Lvl_HexWorld` without `?Map=` then opens the planet instead (`HexLastView::ShouldOpenPlanet`, checked by `AHexWorldMap`, which skips loading a map, and by `AHexMapPlayerController`), and opening a map from the planet writes `bPlanetOpen` false first, or it would come straight back. Each camera keeps the other's fields when it saves.

- The seabed lies `ShallowDepth` or `DeepDepth` below the sea. Water is a cap of rings around the point under the camera, dense close to it and reaching a quarter of the way around, which `AHexPlanet::Tick` turns after the camera has moved. `M_HexPlanetWater` is a separate copy of the open sea of `WATER_CODE` (swell, ripples, colors, the lighter side of waves, glints), kept in `create_planet_assets.py`, so a change to the flat water does not reach it. Noise comes from three planes of the world position blended by the normal (`TRI_NOISE`), waves run along 3D directions with their slope along the surface, the sun comes from the light through `SunDirection`, and the water darkens on the night side. The camera of the planet looks from further than the flat one, so `WaveScale` stretches the swell, chop and ripples, also in `WATER_OFFSET_CODE`, and from a pixel size of `FarCalmStart` the ripples, slope and lighter side of waves fade, as dense waves only shimmer from far away. The cap turns with the camera, so its vertices slide over the waves, and as the surface between them is flat, a wave with few vertices on it pulses in height as the camera scrolls, faster the faster it moves, so the cap is dense (`WaterRings`, `WaterSegments`, `WaterInnerStep`) and `WATER_OFFSET_CODE` blurs the noise that bends the swell to that spacing and fades each wave out of the height as the step of its bent phase between points a spacing apart nears a wavelength (`VertexWaveFadeStart` to `VertexWaveFadeEnd`), and it stays in the normal of the pixels. The noise that bends the swell moves its phase so far that both shaders take it at one mip from any camera (`SwellBendMip`), as a mip chosen by the size of a pixel or the spacing made the waves run as the camera moved, and the patches of tone take theirs at `PatchMip`, as the fine detail of the noise speckled them. `HexPlanetSeaField` writes a cubemap with the depth of the seabed, in blue the ice, in alpha the signed distance to the coast, (distance + 50) / 450 so it goes 50 under the land, a soft minimum (`ShoreSoftness` of `DA_HexPlanet`) of the distances to the land cells within three rings, each a disc as wide as the middles of its sides (`FShoreParams`), which grows evenly inside a cell where a blend between cell centers is flat and further out follows the run of the coast rather than each cape, and in green how much its lines bend around land (their curvature times the distance to the nearest land center, 1 around a lone cape), computed exactly from the discs; near the real coast, which winds across the cells (land rises out of the shallows, the sea floods parts of land cells), the distance moves to be 0 just where the ground crosses the sea: `HexPlanetSurface::Build` gives the points where the edges of its triangles cross the sea by chunk (`FBuild::Coasts`, kept in `AHexPlanet::ChunkCoasts` and brought up to date by a rebuild in its task), each takes the value of the field of the cells there, and a texel takes off those of the points nearby, weighted towards the nearest over `ShoreCoastBlend`, fully within a third of `ShoreCoastFade` of them and fading out by it, so the breakers and their run-up meet the real shore (`AHexPlanet::GetFrozenCells`: frozen sea and land next to it), blended between cell centers by `FHexSphereGrid::FindBlend`, its faces in the GPU order of `GetTexelDirection`. A second cubemap, the shore field (`AHexPlanet::ShoreField`, the second half of the same pixels, so a rebuild carries both), cuts the coast into stretches, one land cell each unless `ShoreStretchJoin` joins two: red is the phase of the stretch of the nearest land cell, from a hash of its first cell, and green how far a texel lies from the end of that stretch / 256, from the bisector towards the nearest land cell of another stretch (`(r2^2 - r1^2) / (2 |c2 - c1|)`), so the breakers fade out there, before the phase blends across it between texels; blue is how far it lies along the coast from the middle of the stretch / 256 (across the mean direction from its cells to the sea), which caps the length of a breaker, and alpha how fast the distance to the coast grows (the length of the gradient of the soft minimum). The frozen sea (blue of the sea field, edge moved by noise) is open, passable water with floes on it: `WATER_CODE` makes it dark and cold (`IceWaterColor`, `IceWaterShare`), fading in over about a cell from the open sea (`IceWaterFade`, wider than the edge of the floes, `IceEdgeSoftness`, so the two waters do not meet on a line) with lower waves (`IceWaveShare`, also in `WATER_OFFSET_CODE`), slush that drifts with it (`IceSlush`), foam where it meets a floe and clearer water over what lies just under it, so the ledge of a floe shows, both by the scene depth behind the water (`IceFoamWidth`, `IceUnderClearness`, `IceClearDepth`), and from far away, where floes are culled, their color (`IceFar*`); all are `Ice*` of `DA_HexPlanetWater`. The floes are instances (`AHexPlanet::UpdateFloes`, `FloeComponents`, a `UHierarchicalInstancedStaticMeshComponent` per mesh of `Floes` of `DA_HexPlanetKit`): every cell of frozen sea (level below 0 and a frozen biome) gets a cover of floes, `FloeEdgeCover` next to open sea growing to `FloeInnerCover` `FloeDenseRings` cells in, moved by a noise (`FloeCoverNoise`), and the largest floe grows from `FloeEdgeSizeMax` to `FloeSizeMax` alike, so the edge of the pack is broken and loose and its inside holds large plates; `FloeTries` sizes, largest first, are thrown at random places in the cell, a floe keeping its center in its cell, `FloeShoreGap` of its radius from a side towards land and `FloeGap` from other floes, and those below `FloeShardSize` take the shard meshes. Each mesh has two components, as the unlit water takes no shadows and hides the deep seabed: floes over the shallows cast shadows (`bFloeShadows`), whose seabed shows them through the water, and floes over the deep sea cast none. They are placed at the start and whenever a rebuild changed the sea field, on the main thread (Terra: about 3,200 floes, 2,000 of them over the shallows, in 5 ms, about 0.23 million triangles; close over the pack at 1280x720 on the machine it was made on they cost about 0.27 ms of GPU with all shadows and within the noise, under 0.1 ms, with the split), and all their values are `Floe*` of `DA_HexPlanet`. `M_HexPlanetFloe` of `create_planet_assets.py` colors their top, sides and what lies under the water, and moves them in its world position offset (`FLOE_OFFSET_CODE`): each drifts, turns back and forth, bobs and rocks at its own pace, so neighbors drift together and apart, which stands in for grinding; past `FloeMotionDistance` they stand still and past `FloeCullDistance` they are not drawn. The slabs and shards come from `generate_planet_kit.py` (`Floe`: a broken, stretched outline, a top of leaning facets, a wide ledge under the water, 1.3 of the outline, and a bottom). The shore has foam (`Shore*`, `Surf*`, `Runup*` and `Roller*` of `DA_HexPlanetWater`). `WATER_CODE` finds how deep the water is over the ground behind it from the scene depth, turned into the depth along the normal of the planet, so it follows the real coast and not the cells, and lays a film of foam at the edge, laced (`ShoreLace`) and never narrower than a few pixels of the change of the depth (`ShoreFoamPixels`), thinner from far away (`ShoreFar*`). Breakers come one by one along the coast: the coast is cut into stretches, one land cell each unless `ShoreStretchJoin` of `DA_HexPlanet` joins two, each breaking at its own phase, which the shore field gives (`ShoreField`, see the sea field), and `SHORE_WAVE_CODE`, shared by the water and `M_HexPlanet`, reads the wave of the stretch in front of a point: every `SurfPeriod` seconds a stretch sends one with `SurfChance`, now and then a roller (`RollerChance`, by `SHORE_HASH` of the wave and the stretch). The wave forms `SurfReach` from land, about one and a half cells, along the distance to the coast in alpha of the sea field (signed, so it goes on under the land), divided by how fast that grows (alpha of the shore field), as close to the shore the land cells around pull the soft minimum different ways and it grows slowly there, so a wave rushed in and swelled at the end; it rolls in at an even speed over `SurfRunShare` of the period as a thin line (`SurfWidthStart` to `SurfWidthEnd` world units, divided out of the distance by how fast it grows on screen, `surfGrad`) that grows from nothing to full white with a short tail of streaks, then over the last `SurfShoreDistance`, two fifths of a cell, speeds up to `SurfShoreSpeed` of its speed by the coast line, as the white water of a broken wave rushes in (a slowdown there, which `SurfShoreSpeed` below 1 still gives, looked wrong; the speed changes evenly with the distance, so `SHORE_WAVE_CODE` places the crest in closed form, `stretchCrest`, and gives back when it reaches a distance, `SHORE_CREST_TIME`), and at full strength rolls on at that pace to the real edge of the water, wherever that lies against the coast line of the field, and on under the land, whose ground hides it. It fades out towards the ends of its stretch (`SurfBorderStart`, `SurfBorderEnd`) and is at most `SurfLength`, a cell, long, however far the wedge of sea in front of its stretch fans out, and at its ends it starts `SurfCurl` further out and catches up by the shore zone, so it bends into a C. Just when it meets the edge of the water its water runs up the beach: `RUNUP_CODE` in `M_HexPlanet` (so `AHexPlanet` gives the surface materials `DA_HexPlanetWater`, the sea field and the shore field too, and `RUNUP_APPLY_CODE` lays its foam and wet sand on after the rivers, which keep them off out to `RiverRunupGap` past their banks, so at a mouth the foam does not roll up a river) finds, for a point of the beach, where the edge of the water lies below it, from the distance to the coast there plus its height over the tangent of the slope of the ground (the normal of the vertex, smooth across triangles), and from when the crest reached that edge the water runs up there fast over `RunupRiseTime` to `RunupHeight` above the sea, `RollerScale` higher for a roller, then slowly back over `RunupFallTime`, a rim of foam at its edge, the foam it left sliding down after it in thinning streaks, and the sand it wet, once the water has slid back off it, darker until it dries (`WetDarkening`; darkened under the sheet of water it looked like a shadow under the foam); timed alike, its foam slides back into the water over the edge (`BackwashDepth`). So the foam is born just where and when the wave meets the shore, whichever side of the coast line of the field the real shore lies; a start at one time for the whole stretch, when the crest crossed that line, was early or late by up to a second or two.
 Earlier tries taught what to avoid: fronts by depth covered the shallow shelf in blotches, as it is not much deeper than they ran; the noise of the texture of water stays blotchy even at a coarse mip; a time shift that changed along the coast faster than the phase changes across it made waves run along the coast or out to sea; a lag of the ends that changed faster than the distance turned arcs across the coast; and with one slow shift the whole coast broke as one wide front, hence the stretches. The breakers fade out where the lines of the distance wrap around a cape (`SurfCapeStart`, `SurfCapeEnd` against the green of the sea field), as there they would run across the coast. The lagoon colors and the lower waves near land (`LagoonDistance*`) also use that distance. The same depth puts foam on the wide ledge of each floe (`IceFoam`, `IceFoamWidth`), laced by the slush, and the shores of the frozen sea get the film and fewer breakers. On the machine it was made on it cost nothing measurable at 1280x720 (over a coast 3.40 to 3.67 ms of GPU between runs, 3.56 with the stretches, over the pack 3.96), and the sea field with the shore field takes about 0.27 s to build on Terra, 0.24 without the real coast and 0.16 without the stretches. - A map is a folder in `Content/Data/Maps`, read at runtime, so it is not an asset: packaging stages all of `Content/Data` as files (`DirectoriesToAlwaysStageAsUFS` in `DefaultGame.ini`), and `DefaultEditorPerProjectUserSettings.ini` keeps the editor from importing its images as textures (`AutoReimportDirectorySettings`). It holds `map.json` (`FHexMapManifest`: version, name, description, size, `sortOrder`), `levels.png`, an optional `biomes.png` and an optional `rivers.json`. Dense layers where every hex has a value are images, and sparse things with an order or values of their own, such as rivers and later objects, are JSON, one file per kind, which Git can merge. One pixel is one hex, and rows are odd-r offset, so odd rows shift half a hex east. The game finds maps by scanning `Content/Data/Maps`, and the lowest `sortOrder` is the default.
- A hex is a pair of a level (shape and height) and a biome (surface). Hills are level 1 with some biome, and ice is a biome on a water level, not a level of its own. Each is its own asset, because binary assets cannot be merged: `UHexLevelConfig` in `Content/HexMap/Levels` and `UHexBiomeConfig` in `Content/HexMap/Biomes`, found through the asset registry, so adding one needs no shared file. `Tools/create_biome_assets.py` creates the biomes and resets their values. A biome chooses the texture layer of the terrain, the rock of mountains and their snow line. Move cost is the level's times the biome's, a level allows crossing and a biome can only forbid it. Nothing uses these yet except the debug panel.
- A river runs through hex centers. `rivers.json` lists each river with its hexes as `[column, row]` in the order it flows, from its source to the hex it ends in, and optionally its `name`, `meander` and `seed`. Every river has one width, `RiverWidth` of `DA_HexMap`, as bridges must fit any of them, so `width`, `widthVariation` and `pools` are errors. `HexMapLoader::ParseRivers` keeps them as `FHexMapData::RiverPaths` and finds the river of each hex (`FHexRiver` in `FHexMapData::Rivers`, which movement will use). A river ends at the water next to its last hex, or in another river that flows through its last hex, which makes it a tributary. Each hex flows on in one river only, so rivers may run side by side. The hexes of a river must touch, never go up a level, and not be water; the error names the river and the hex. `Size` counts the hexes upstream, for the width. A river is never on a level with meshes, such as mountains (`HexMapLoader::ValidateRiverLevels`), so it starts next to them. Movement cost (entering a river hex) waits for a movement system. `Content/Data/Maps/RiverTest` shows them.
- `HexMeshBuilder::BuildRivers` runs once per map after the cliffs: it makes a line (`FHexRiverLine`) for each river of the file through hex centers and side middles, and a tributary ends on the line of the larger river. Points wind by a field of offsets of the position, moved by the seed of the river and scaled by its `meander` (`RiverMeander`), are rounded by corner cutting and spaced evenly, and the line goes on into the water past its mouth. It narrows only towards its source and where it ends in another river (`RiverTaperLength`). The water of a river (`Surface`) lies `RiverBankHeight` below the ground averaged along it, so bumps do not drag it down, follows it down and never rises, and a tributary never lies below the river it meets. `FTerrainWriter::ApplyRivers` lays a flat floor `RiverDepth` below the water out to the foot of the bank, just inside the edge of the water, and the face of the bank straight up from there to its top, `RiverBankLean` past the edge, which lies `RiverBankHeight` above the water on both sides, also where the ground falls faster than the water, so every bank is as high, and from there the land goes back to its own height over `RiverBankSettle`. The seabed past a mouth keeps its own height and color, as the sea covers the river there. It runs before `ApplyCoast`. The bank is the terrain itself: `FTerrainWriter::AddFineTriangle` halves a triangle of the lattice into four, down to an eighth of a lattice step (`RiverSplit`), as long as an edge of it crosses the top or the foot of a bank or lies on its face (`NeedsSplit`, which depends only on the edge, so the triangles on both sides split it alike), so the land falls sharply at the line of the bank and the rest of the lattice stays coarse. An edge that is not split keeps its middle on the edge and its halves are never split, so a triangle that is split meets one that is not without a crack. A point less than about half a fine step (`RiverBankSnap`) from the top or the foot of a bank moves onto it, so the bank runs along the river and not in steps of the fine lattice, and every edge that crosses the line has an end on it. The top and the foot round off over `RiverBankRounding` (`SmoothMin`), so the bank has no hard angle, and points on a bank measure their normals over a tiny step (`RiverBankNormalStep`), so they take that rounded slope right where they lie, while the other points of the lattice keep theirs, as every chunk computes them alike. The sand of the bank in `M_HexTerrain` is laid from three sides, as on the steep face a texture from above would stretch into streaks. How much of the bed a place is goes into the alpha of the terrain vertex colors, which `M_HexTerrain` draws as light earthy sand, a little darker on the floor, and on a strip of the land past the top, so the grass does not reach the edge of the bank: past the top the alpha falls evenly with the distance over `RiverBankColorReach`, which any triangle draws right, and `TERRAIN_CODE` ends the sand at a width that winds by noise (`bankRim`, with `bankTopColor` and `bankColorReach` repeating the values of the code). `HexMeshBuilder::BuildRiverWater` lays one ribbon mesh for all rivers (`RiverWater` of `AHexWorldMap`) at the height of their water, one strip of triangles across, as the water is flat across, and a little wider than the river, so their edge goes under the face of the bank, which makes the edge of the water. Its UVs go across and along, and its vertex colors carry rapids from how fast the water falls, foam near the sea and fades at the ends, which `M_HexRiver` (unlit and translucent, from `build_hex_materials.py`, `RIVER_CODE`) draws as flowing water. Where a river reaches the sea its water lies just under the sea. The river values are in `DA_HexMap`, and the lattice step is about the width of a small river, so the bed is rough.

- Hexes are pointy-top. `FHexCoord` is axial and shared code for any hex grid, so the battle map should reuse it. North on the map is +X and columns go towards +Y.
- `HexMeshBuilder` makes one continuous surface. Each hex is subdivided on a triangular lattice that covers the whole map, and height, color and normal depend only on the horizontal position: levels blended by the three nearest hex centers, plus per-level noise (`BumpHeight`, `RidgeHeight`). Chunks therefore join without seams. The `Seamless` and `ChunksMatchFullMap` tests check this.
- `AHexWorldMap` keeps only the chunks near the view, found from the screen corners on the ground. It builds them with `UE::Tasks`, and adds at most a few per tick. Everything a build reads lives in the shared `FHexTerrainSource`, never in the actor. The terrain has no LODs, which relies on `MaxZoom` in `AHexCameraPawn` keeping most of the map out of view. The mountain meshes are Nanite, which the import turns on by itself, so `stat unit` does not count their triangles in `Prims`.
- A level can have a ready-made mesh (`FeatureMesh` in its level asset) for what a height map cannot do, such as mountains. `AHexWorldMap` places it on every hex of that level as instances per chunk, fitted to `FeatureWidth` and `FeatureHeight` whatever its size, with `FeatureSink` hiding where it meets the terrain. The terrain never adjusts to a mesh beyond its flat level height yet. `Content/HexMap/Meshes/Rock` is a placeholder copied from an engine template.
- A level can instead have meshes that join across the sides of hexes (`JoinedMeshes` in its level asset), which replace `FeatureMesh`. `HexMeshBuilder::GetJoinedPiece` turns the mask of same-level neighbors into one of 14 meshes and a turn of 60 degrees. Straight lines of hexes run along three axes, all made by mask 9 turned, and a north-south line is a zigzag made by mask 5. `JoinedMeshVariants` holds other versions with the same sides (`EXTRA_VARIANTS` in `blender_build_mountains.py`), and each hex keeps one. A symmetric set of neighbors also turns by hex (`GetJoinedExtraTurns`). Every cut across a ridge has the profile of a side (`get_ridge_profile`), so a straight range looks like one mountain, and away from the sides towards a mountain it blends into `get_free_profile`, a concave slope that reaches the foot only at the side, so a lone mountain fills its hex. They keep their size and pivot, so `FeatureWidth`, `FeatureHeight` and `FeatureSink` do not apply. They fill the whole hex: a side towards another level lies at one height, `JoinedMeshFootHeight`. `HexMeshBuilder` builds no terrain in such a hex, and the land around it keeps its own height, losing its noise over `JoinedMeshFootBlend`. `HexMeshBuilder::BuildTerrainHeights` writes the height of the terrain at its vertices around such hexes to a texture (`FHexTerrainHeights`), and `M_HexMountain` moves the low part of the mesh by it (`MOUNTAIN_OFFSET_CODE`), so its edge lies exactly on the terrain whatever level the neighbor has, and one set of meshes fits them all. A group of such hexes grows into one massif: `HexMeshBuilder::BuildMassifDepths` counts how far each lies inside its group, and the same offset raises the mesh by `JoinedMeshMassifRise` and stretches it above its foot by `JoinedMeshMassifStep` for each hex inwards, up to `JoinedMeshMassifMaxSteps`, blended between hexes so neighbors still meet. Where two such hexes meet a lower hex, each mesh goes on past its side, halfway to the center of the lower hex (`get_extensions`), so a range stands on a straight line, and they share the line that continues the side between them. The terrain stays under that base, which is laid on it. `JoinedNotchMesh` stays empty. The mountains in `Content/HexMap/Meshes/Mountains` come from `Tools/HexMountains`, and their shared values from `mountain_params.json`: the generator and Blender build the meshes from it, and `assign_hex_mountains.py` copies `JoinedMeshRadius`, `JoinedMeshFootHeight` and `JoinedMeshRimFadeHeight` into the level asset, from which the game and the materials take them. Change that file, not those values. With the editor closed, `rebuild_hex_mountains.ps1` runs `blender_build_mountains.py` with Blender 5.2 (`blender --background --factory-startup --python <file>`), then `import_hex_mountains.py`, `Tools/build_hex_materials.py` and `assign_hex_mountains.py` with the commandlet below, and stops at the first failure. `generate_hex_mountains.py` holds the shapes, `TOP_STYLES` and the ridge line, and `blender_mountain_trial.py` adds erosion and can render a test group. The build fails if neighbors would not match in height or normal, and the import reports `wrong_sides`, which must be 0. Detail is baked into one mask atlas (red is the shade of the rock, green moves the snow line) and one normal atlas used by `M_HexMountain`.
- `Tools/build_hex_materials.py` builds `M_HexTerrain`, `M_HexMountain` and `M_HexWater`, so edit that file and not the materials. Every value of their look is in `DA_HexMapSurface` (`UHexMapSurfaceConfig`: layers, grid, beach, river banks, mountains, snow, moss, spray, cliffs) and `DA_HexMapWater` (`UHexMapWaterConfig`: colors, swell and chop as (frequency, speed, slope, height), calm, waves of the shore, rollers, edge foam, cliff foam, surge, glints, ice, floes, cracks, plates, rivers), referenced by `DA_HexMap` and created by the script only when missing. A value is named the same in the HLSL, and `add_config_params` gives a material a parameter for each name its code holds, with the default of the class read from the header, while `AHexWorldMap::ApplyLook` gives every material of the map both configs (`HexMaterialConfig::Apply`), the sun, `WaterHeight` and `RiverBankTopColor` and `RiverBankColorReach` of `Tuning`, once when it is made. They draw the grid and find the three hexes around a pixel in Custom HLSL from world positions, which repeats `FHexLayout`, so change both together. This is why `AHexWorldMap` stays at the world origin. They read each hex from `HexData`, a transient texture with one pixel per hex from `HexMapLoader::BuildHexData`, and blend its layer from the arrays `TA_Biome_Color` (height in alpha) and `TA_Biome_Normal`. The foot of a mountain computes the same surface as the terrain, which is why they meet without a seam. The code must use `SampleGrad` with the `PosDdx` and `PosDdy` inputs, as ray tracing shaders reject `Sample` and a material that fails to compile silently renders as a gray default, so look for `Failed to compile Material` in the log. The terrain normal is in world space, as the terrain has no tangents. Vertex colors and the `TerrainColor` of levels are no longer drawn.
- Water is one plane above the terrain. Its look comes from `HexData`, not from scene depth, so it does not change with the camera angle. Level -2 has `bHidesTerrain`: it is fully opaque over its whole hex, and `HexMeshBuilder` builds terrain only in such hexes next to something else. Level -1 shows the terrain under it, at the `WaterOpacity` of its biome (`DA_Biome_Shoals` is clearer than `DA_Biome_Water`), and all water grows opaque with the depth of the seabed under it, which `BuildShoreField` writes blurred into the alpha of the shore field, between `WaterClearDepth` and `WaterOpaqueDepth` of `DA_HexMap`. A soft film of foam (`WaterEdgeFoam`, `WaterEdgeFoamWidth`) always lies where the water touches anything and each swash runs in to the edge as a bright rim and goes back as a thin sheet that thins into streaks, each stretch of coast at its own time, three of them a third of a cycle apart, so one always goes back while the next comes in. Its distance from the edge is how deep the ground lies divided by how fast it falls, from `ShoreHeight`, a second transient texture of the shore field (`FHexShoreField::HeightPixels`, 16 bits, as 8 make its lines wobble over flat ground) with the unblurred height of the ground above the water, whose filtering finds the edge between its pixels, so it is the same from any side of the camera and on any coast, and flat shallows count as far; it is clearer in some stretches and fainter in others, never gone. Under the water `M_HexMountain` takes the same soft color of the seabed as `M_HexTerrain`. `bFrozen` on a biome (`DA_Biome_Ice`) puts ice on the water.
- Waves need no map of currents. `HexMeshBuilder::BuildShoreField` finds the distance from all water to the real edge of the land and the cliffs at startup (`FHexShoreField`), and `AHexWorldMap::CreateShoreField` gives it to `M_HexWater` as a transient texture. Fronts of waves run along lines of the blurred distance towards the shore, foam breaks on their crests, and the same distance colors the water from lagoon to open sea. Water is a grid of fixed vertex count, which `AHexWorldMap::UpdateWaterGrid` moves and scales over the view, and `M_HexWater` moves its vertices with `WATER_OFFSET_CODE`, whose waves must match the swell in `WATER_CODE`. It hides what lies past the edge of the map, and does not move under ice, whose edge `WATER_CODE` breaks into floes near real water, while further in some hexes are hard plates of ice, so they never look open, with small waves running in to it. Glints of the sun are sparkles that drift with the water and last for seconds, and the camera only decides where the path of the sun lies over them, so they do not flicker as it moves. The lighter side of waves and the drifts of snow take the direction of the sun of the level, which `AHexWorldMap::ApplyLook` gives as `SunDirection`. Over shallows, by the depth of the seabed in the shore field, the water is calmer (`shallowCalm`): smaller waves, ripples and swell in `WATER_CODE` and `WATER_OFFSET_CODE`, fewer glints and less foam of waves, and the film at the edge is a smooth sheet with a soft rim, without the veins of the lace, while the swash at the edge and the foam of cliffs stay. Now and then a single wave rolls in from `RollerDistance`, and `RollerRarity` sets how seldom. A wave's foam comes only in patches one to three hexes wide, new ones for each wave. It is born close in, seldom as far as a hex out, at its own place for each wave, and rolls in. The blue of the shore field is how near a cliff or the side of a joined mesh towards water (`JoinedShoreFoamReach`, and the notch between two of them that their base fills) it is, with the distance to the rock also taken from the scene depth, as a mountain stands further out in the water than the field knows, and `M_HexMountain` throws the same spray up its rock as `M_HexCliff`, which adds layers of foam at its foot, and a surge (`SURGE_CODE`, shared by `WATER_CODE`, `WATER_OFFSET_CODE` and `CLIFF_CODE`) rises fast and falls slowly in patches a few hexes wide, heaving the water and throwing spray up the rock. The bottom seen through water is blurred color in `M_HexTerrain`. Waves running up the beach are in `M_HexTerrain`, from its height above `WaterHeight`.
- A side between land and water, other than of a joined mesh, has a cliff if their levels differ by at least `CliffMinLevelDifference`, and a smaller difference is a beach (`HexMeshBuilder::IsCliffSide`). `HexMeshBuilder::BuildCliffs` runs once per map: it joins those sides into lines of coast, rounds them with a few passes of corner cutting, and moves them out to sea with noise, so they do not show the hexes. A hex with a joined mesh never has a cliff: the seabed next to it rises to just under the water at its side (`JoinedShoreDepth`) and falls to its own depth by the middle of the hex, its mesh follows that down under the water, and in the material that slope is bare rock. Its mesh is built along that line and each cut blends by noise between ledges, a leaning wall and a slope (`GetCliffReach`), as the camera sees little of a sheer face. It fades out over `CliffEndLength` where it meets a beach. `AddCliffRange` gives each chunk the parts of its hexes, as section 1 of its terrain component with `M_HexCliff`. The terrain follows the signed distance to that line (`ApplyCoast`, found through `FHexCliffData::Nearby`): flat at `CliffTop` inland, a little lower under the top of the mesh (`CliffLipClearance`), falling behind it, and at the seabed in front of it, which the reach of the cuts must stay ahead of. The inner edge of that top always goes under the terrain, and in a tight bend it reaches inland only until another part of the coast is closer, or it would fold over and show as dark cracks.
- The texture layers are an asset for an artist, `DA_HexTerrainLayers` in `Content/HexMap/Textures` (`UHexTerrainLayersConfig`, referenced by `TerrainLayers` of `HexMapSettings`): the index of a layer there is the layer number of the biome assets (`TerrainLayer`, `MountainRockLayer`, `PlanetBeachLayer`, the tables of `BEACH_LAYERS` and `ROCK_LAYERS`), so new layers go at the end. Each layer names its `Color` (sRGB; its alpha is the height when there is no `Height`), an optional `Height`, a `Normal` in the DirectX convention (`bNormalGreenUp` flips an OpenGL one), the values the planet draws it with (see `BIOME_CODE`) and, for a rock, its `ScreeLayer` and `GravelLayer` by name. Build Texture Arrays on the asset (`BuildTextureArrays`, editor only) resizes every layer to `ArraySize` (1024), puts a height into the alpha of its color and flips a normal in memory, and writes them as the slices of `ColorArray` and `NormalArray` (`TA_Biome_Color`, `TA_Biome_Normal`), which then name no source textures, so press it after changing a texture and save; no packed texture is saved. The sources stay at 2K, so `ArraySize` 2048 brings their detail back: 1024 looked almost the same from the closest camera on 2026-11-07, and takes about 40 and 48 MB in LFS for each build where 2048 took 150 and 190, and about 48 MB of the GPU with their 11 mips (`TMGS_FromTextureGroup`, which `import_biome_textures.py` sets, as the factory makes an array without mips, whose ground grained from far away). It leaves the arrays alone when no source pixel nor the size changed (`BuiltSourceHash`), as each holds a copy of every layer. The noise of the water and ice of both maps (`TRI_NOISE`, `SHORE_NOISE` and the flat water) is `T_HexNoise` in `Content/HexMap/Textures`, given to the materials as `HexNoise`, which `build_biome_textures.py` writes from the height of its snow and `import_biome_textures.py` imports, so every layer, snow too, can be replaced. Layer 18, `CaveFlagstones`, the worn flagstones and hex tiles of the paths of the underground, is appended past the photo-scanned ones and found by name (`APPENDED_LAYERS` of both scripts): `build_biome_textures.py -- CaveFlagstones` writes only it (`make_flagstones`: slabs of a periodic Voronoi with a patch of hex tiles, bevelled and worn joints, a few cracked and about one in eight missing) and `import_biome_textures.py appended` imports only it, adds the layer when it is missing and builds the arrays, so the other generated layers and `T_HexNoise` stay byte for byte. Nothing draws it since the flagstones of the passages were taken out on 2026-11-10; it stays in the arrays until they are built again without it. Layers 0 to 9 come from `Tools/HexTextures` without any downloads: run `build_biome_textures.py` with Blender (only numpy is needed), then `import_biome_textures.py` with the commandlet, which, once the asset exists, imports only the layers whose color there is still a `T_Biome_` one and builds the arrays through it. The rocks (2 gray, 5 red, 6 ochre, 7 basalt) with their scree and gravel (10 to 17) and the snow (3) draw photo-scanned CC0 sets listed in `texture_sources.json`, from Poly Haven, the snow from ambientCG; grass, sand, swamp, tundra and steppe keep the generated layers, as real sets for them (tried on 2026-11-07) looked worse. `fetch_texture_sources.ps1` downloads them at 2K to `Saved/TextureSources/<source>` (not committed; ambientCG zips are unpacked there), and `import_texture_sources.py`, run with the commandlet, imports them to `Content/HexMap/Textures/Layers`, creates the asset if missing and puts each set, with its `tileSize`, `tint`, scree and gravel, into its layer only while that layer has no color or still the generated one, so what an artist changed stays, and deletes the generated textures that no layer uses any more; then `build_hex_materials.py` and `create_planet_assets.py`. `LAYERS` in the scripts is the order of the generated layers in `Tools/create_biome_assets.py`. The flat map still draws the snow layer on its mountains and ice (`M_HexMountain`, `M_HexWater`). `Content/Data/Maps/MountainTest` shows every kind of chain on bands of biomes.
 The alpha of the snow layer is also the noise of water and ice, so its relief goes only into its normal, which `M_HexMountain` also draws on the snow of mountains.
- `DA_HexInput` (`UHexInputConfig`) in `Content/HexMap/Input` holds the input actions and the mapping context `IMC_HexMap`, which holds all the keys, and `HexMapSettings` references it. `Tools/create_input_assets.py` creates them and resets the keys. A key with a modifier, such as Shift+F, is F chorded with `IA_HexShift` (`UInputTriggerChordAction`), and Enhanced Input then blocks plain F while Shift is held. A key held with two, such as Ctrl+Shift+1, has a chord for each and must come before the same key held with one of them in the context, as Enhanced Input evaluates the chorded mappings in its order and blocks the later ones of a key while an earlier one fires (the `HexMap.Config.Input` test checks the bookmarks); the legend joins keys of one character held with the same keys, as Ctrl+0-9 (`JoinKeyNames`). `SHexMapMenu` closes with the keys of `MapMenuAction`, read from the mapping context, because the viewport ignores input while the menu is open. That action needs a key besides Escape, which stops a play session in the editor.
- `Tools/setup_hex_lighting.py` sets the lights of `Lvl_HexWorld`, whose fixed exposure is `ExposureBias` of `DA_HexCamera`. Water is unlit, so its colors change with that exposure too. The camera lowers from `CameraPitch` to `CloseCameraPitch` as it zooms in.
- All movement of `AHexCameraPawn` goes through `ScrollVelocity`, which gives the inertia. The right mouse button and Q/E turn the camera up to `MaxCameraTurn`, and it turns back when they are released. The free camera (C, or the console variable `hex.Camera` 0 or 1) turns all the way around and stays turned, for looking at the map from any side. `bStartFree` in `DA_HexCamera` picks the mode the camera starts in, which is also the usual view of its status panel, unless `hex.Camera` was set from the console or the key, so a new map keeps the chosen mode. The console command `hex.FocusHex <Column> <Row> [Yaw] [Zoom]` glides the camera until that hex, at the height of its level or water, is in the center of the screen, where `-` keeps the yaw or zoom and a yaw makes the camera free; the middle mouse button does the same for the hex under the mouse (`AHexCameraPawn::FocusHex`), and any movement stops the glide. `hex.CopyCamera` (Ctrl+C) copies to the clipboard `hex.LoadMap <Folder> | hex.SetCamera <X> <Y> <Yaw> <Zoom>`, which the console runs as two commands, to bring the view back in another run (on the command line `-ExecCmds` needs a comma in place of `|`). `hex.LoadMap` keeps a map that is already open, and `hex.SetCamera` puts the camera there at once, waiting in `PendingPlacement` until the level has opened again and the camera has its map.
- Each mode of the view has its own panel of settings in `SHexDebugOverlay`, shown while it differs from the usual view and for a few seconds after a change. `AHexMapPlayerController::AddViewStatus` adds one, as it does for the debug view of `AHexWorldMap` and the camera mode of `AHexCameraPawn`. `Curve_EdgeScroll` in `DA_HexCamera` sets the scroll strength inside the screen edge zone, the console variable `hex.EdgeScroll 0` turns that scroll off, and `Tools/create_edge_scroll_curve.py` creates it.
- A triangle shows on the side where `(C - A) x (B - A)` points.

Known cosmetic issue: the walls along the map edge show through the water as small shards.

### Module

## Unreal MCP

`.mcp.json` connects Claude Code to the engine's experimental Unreal MCP plugin at `http://127.0.0.1:8000/mcp`.

- The server runs inside the editor, so the `unreal-mcp` tools are available only while the editor is open.
- `bAutoStartServer` in `Config/DefaultEditorPerProjectUserSettings.ini` starts the server with the editor.
- Tools are discovered through `list_toolsets` and `describe_toolset`, then run through `call_tool` with the toolset name and the short tool name.
- `ProgrammaticToolset.execute_tool_script` batches many calls, and inside it tools use their full names. A Custom material node starts with one input, so its `Inputs` array must grow one element at a time before the names are set.
- More toolsets live in `Engine/Plugins/Experimental/Toolsets` in the engine folder. Enable them in `HexHero.uproject` with `TargetAllowList` set to `Editor`, and restart the editor.

A `-game` run compiles the shaders of a changed material on demand and draws the default grey material meanwhile, and one that quits within a minute never caches them, so after rebuilding a material let a run go on for a minute or two before judging how it looks.

Assets can also be edited without an open editor, by running a Python script with `UnrealEditor-Cmd.exe <uproject> -run=pythonscript -script=<file> -unattended -nullrhi`. `unreal.log` output appears only in `Saved/Logs/HexHero.log`, not on stdout. One call of `MaterialEditingLibrary.delete_all_material_expressions` deletes only about half of the expressions in this engine, and the rest stay in the asset unconnected, so the material scripts call it until none is left (`clear_expressions`).

## Version control

`.uasset` and `.umap` files are binary and tracked with Git LFS, so they cannot be merged. Commit before bulk editor changes. Changes that are unsaved in the editor are not on disk and will not be committed.
